> Companion to [evolution-rework.md](evolution-rework.md), whose Phases 1 and 2 built the genome, the archive and the live policy. This doc replaces its Phase 3.

Training runs locally, headless, on every core. A genome is trained by natural selection in whole worlds: the trainer runs many short worlds on fixed seeds, offers every agent that lived to the archive by the live policy's fitness, and starts the next worlds from the archive. Drills are the exam. Each drill stages one situation (food in view, prey in reach, a hunter closing) in a small arena and counts how often a genome does the thing a viewer would expect. A drill score is a detector: it never enters fitness, merit, mate rank or archive rank ([principles.md](../principles.md), Measure scenes, reward them almost never). Easier worlds come first in a curriculum, so a skill is reachable before the full world tests it; selection in every stage is still survival and offspring.

## Drills

`js/evo/drill-scenarios.js` (new) exports `DRILLS`, a frozen list in the shape of `SCENARIOS` in `js/evo/memory-probe-scenarios.js`: each drill has a `name`, the `scene` it detects, the subject `spec`, `needsHunter`, and `plan(rng, view)` returning a `Plan` that `playTrial` in `js/evo/memory-probe-run.js` runs unchanged with the `intact` control. Placement helpers `heading` and `around` and the arena constants (`CENTRE_X`, `CENTRE_Y`, the 3000 x 4000 arena) are reused from `memory-probe-scenarios.js`. The staged world keeps no refuges (`refugeCount` 0), so the `refugeSense` inputs read 0 in every drill; the brain layout is `pooled-v5` and champion files are checked against it by `validateChampion`, so a champion from an older layout is rejected, not drilled.

- Each drill runs `--trials` (32) trials from the drill's seeded stream (`fork(createRng(seed), 'drill:<name>')`), so a run replays from seed and subject.
- A genome's score per drill is its win share. Its **edge** is the win share minus that of a random brain of the same specialisation run on the same trials (`randomWeights` from `js/evo/mutation.js`; the mean of `RANDOM_BRAINS` (4) random brains drawn from `fork(createRng(seed), 'drill-random:<name>')`, so one unlucky brain does not set the bar). The reference controller `referenceSubject('memoryless')` from `js/evo/memory-probe-references.js` is printed beside it as the skilled bar.
- A drill whose subject `spec` does not match a genome's specialisation is skipped for that genome and reported as `-`.

`npm run drill -- --champions <dir> | --archive <file> | --policy <name>` (`scripts/drill.mjs`, new; `--drills a,b` (default all), `--seed <n>` (1), `--trials <n>` (32), `--config <file.json>`, `--json`, `--out <path>`, `--workers <n>`). `--champions` loads files through `loadChampions` in `scripts/memory-probe.mjs`; `--archive` takes each specialisation's elite with the highest lower bound; `--policy` drills the `--count` (default 8) best archive elites of a fresh 20000-tick world of that policy, run through the eval worker with `saveChampions`, so the policy must keep an archive (`map-elites-v6`). It prints one row per genome: win share and edge per drill, and the mean edge. Trials run on `scripts/lib/worker-pool.mjs`, in index order, so `--workers 1` and `--workers 8` print the same table. Logs go to `.sim/logs/drill-<seed>x<trials>.jsonl` (`DrillTrial` debug per trial, `DrillResult` info per genome and drill).

## Trainer

`npm run train -- --generations <g> [--episodes <e>] [--workers <n>] [--curriculum <file>] [--resume <archive.json>] [--checkpoint-every <n>] --out <dir>` (`scripts/train.mjs`, new, with `scripts/train-worker.mjs`):

- **Policy and fitness.** The trainer runs the live policy (`LIVE_POLICY` in `js/evo/policies/index.js`, `map-elites-v6` today) and scores offers with the fitness that policy names, through two options `createMapElitesV1` takes: `archive` (the frozen copy to seed from, restored from `snapshotArchive` / `restoreArchive` in `js/evo/archive-snapshot.js`, plain typed arrays that cross to a worker without the JSON codec) and `onOffer(item, tick)` (takes each offer instead of `offer(archive, item)`, so an episode never changes an archive). The trainer's archive is `createTrainerArchive` in `js/evo/trainer.js`: `createArchive` with the live descriptor and `nCap` `TRAINER_N_CAP` (8).
- **Episodes.** Episode `k` of generation `g` runs on `evals/train-seeds.json` (new: 256 seeds, disjoint from `evals/seeds.json`) entry `(g x e + k) mod 256`, for `EPISODE_TICKS` (60000) ticks, with `initialAgents` 100. Its initial agents and floor entrants come from a frozen copy of the archive taken at the start of the generation. Generation 0 starts from random brains; no scripted controller seeds the archive (user, 2026-10-17).
- **Offers.** The policy's own offer path decides what is offered (a mature death of an established genome, with the proof rule of natural-selection.md), and the trainer keeps the offers whose death tick is at least `OFFER_FROM_TICK` (10000). Agents still alive at the last tick are not offered: the observer scores fitness at death, and a live agent has no death-log row. (Delegated change from "born after 10000, living agents offered"; see Decisions.)
- **Merge.** `runEpisode` in `js/evo/trainer.js` returns the offers in the order the policy made them, each tagged with `archiveRef` and `isClone`, and the frozen copy's clone cursors; `mergeEpisode` applies them in episode-index order and takes the last episode's cursors, so `--workers 1` and `--workers 32` write byte-identical archives. `--episodes` defaults to 32. Three flags exist for tests and smoke runs: `--config <file>` (one config for every generation, instead of a curriculum), `--ticks <n>` (episode ticks, default 60000, also the checkpoint eval ticks) and `--offer-from <tick>`.
- **Curriculum.** `--curriculum <file>` names a JSON list of stages, each `{ "name", "config", "generations" }`, run in order on one archive. `js/evo/curriculum.js` (`planStages`, `stageAt`; the last stage always runs to the end of the run, and a stage the run never reaches is dropped) lays them out. `evals/train-curriculum.json` holds `nursery` (`evals/micro-world.json`, 20 generations), `hunt` (`evals/train-hunt-world.json`, 20 generations; the old `evals/predator-world.json` no longer passes `createConfig`, its `hashCellSize` is below the largest radius) and `world` (the default config, the rest of `--generations`). Config paths are relative to the repository root. Without `--curriculum` every generation runs the default config. The archive records each stage's `rulesHash` in `stages`; `--resume` checks the `layout` and the current stage's `rulesHash`.
- **Checkpoints.** Every `--checkpoint-every` (10) generations the trainer picks champions as `train:export` would, without writing them, runs every drill on them (seed 1, 32 trials) and a 4-seed eval (`evals/seeds.json` entries 33 to 36, 50000 ticks): the same episode as training, on the current stage's config, seeded from the archive and offering nothing, so its metrics are of the archive's founders and are not comparable across stages. The last generation always checkpoints. `curvePoint` in `js/evo/learning-curve.js` appends a row to `<dir>/curve.jsonl`: `generation`, `stage`, `trainTicks`, `wallSeconds`, `coverage`, `qdScore`, `best` (each specialisation's best lower bound), `drill` (each drill's mean edge over the champions it applies to) and `drillEdge` (their mean), `eval.food_energy_per_1k`, `eval.births_per_1k`, `eval.fed_median_lifespan`, `eval.catch_rate`, `eval.escape_rate`. Checkpoints only read the archive.
- **Plateau.** `npm run train:curve -- <dir>` prints the curve and the plateau generation from `findPlateau`: the first checkpoint after which `qdScore`, `eval.food_energy_per_1k` and the mean drill edge each rise by less than 5% over the next 3 checkpoints, or "no plateau yet".
- **Output.** After each generation `<dir>/archive-<g>.json`, and a `TrainGeneration` log line (generation, stage, coverage, qdScore, wall seconds, CPU share). A failed episode stops the run with exit 1 and `TrainEpisodeFailed`.
- **Export.** `npm run train:export -- --archive <file> --out <dir> [--config <file>] [--label <name>]` (`scripts/train-export.mjs`) writes one champion file per specialisation (the elite with the highest lower bound, `exportBestPerSpec` in `js/evo/champion-export.js`) and an `index.json`. The files are stamped with the `rulesHash` of `--config` (default: the default world), the world they will be played in, not of the curriculum stage that trained them; the layout, version and descriptor are still checked. These files are what `npm run drill -- --champions`, `npm run league` and `npm run probe:memory` read.

## From outcome to task

Every training run, drill run and reflex run ends with this record, written in the doc of the work it judges (this doc for the trainer) before the next run starts. A finding that skips it does not become a task.

1. **Observation.** The detector numbers: drill win shares and edges, reflex pass shares and their bars, curve metrics, with the seeds, trials and the arm that produced them. A number with no bar beside it (random brains, the memoryless reference) is not an observation.
2. **Theory.** The mechanism that would explain it, as one sentence a fixture can falsify. How nature meets the same problem comes first ([principles.md](../principles.md), "How the principles are used"), and the doc names the biology. A theory about what the brain does (never bites, always bites) is checked with a diagnostic before any run that depends on it.
3. **Principle check.** A table row per theory naming the scenes and selection rules it serves and each of these it must not break. One failed line refuses the theory.
    - Detector, not reward: no fitness, merit, archive-rank, offer-filter or mate-rank term pays for an act or for a drill or reflex score ("measure scenes, reward them almost never"; selection rule 9).
    - The hunt chain fails closed: a chase that never bites or a bite that bounces stays a failed gene (rule 6); nothing is rescued, restocked or pardoned (rules 1 and 4).
    - Senses stay local: the brain can sense what the lesson needs (a ray class, a body input), and no input is added to fit a task.
    - No scripted mode, no timer, no restocking, and no free action ("What to refuse").
    - The change is to the world a stage runs in (a config, a curriculum), never to who breeds.
4. **Prediction and detector.** Written before the run: the detector (a drill, a reflex case, a curve metric or an `eval.*` metric), its direction and size, and the minimum evidence. Training outcomes use at least 3 training-seed offsets per arm and 32 drill trials per genome; the reflex case runs at 64 trials; a single archive is a lead, not a verdict. A claim on a world metric uses 16 seeds and `eval:compare` ([run-evolution-loop](../../.claude/skills/run-evolution-loop/SKILL.md)).
5. **Task rows.** Each surviving theory becomes `[judgement]` or `[mechanical]` rows in this doc's Task breakdown under a new phase, with a Chunks table. A diagnostic that tells two theories apart is a row before the run it gates. Code, runs and the final write-up are separate rows; a long run is never in a code chunk ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), "Delegating to subagents").

A refused theory is written into Decisions with the line it failed and the reason. The run's result closes the record: a final row records whether the prediction held and appends the next record's tasks, so the loop continues from data.

**Conclusion of iteration 2.** Only the combination worked: the small dense hunt arena (prey stays in view) on top of a nursery floor (a foraging base keeps the population up), with a long hunt stage. Each lever alone did nothing for the drill (a, b, c all 0.00), so they are not additive in an obvious way; the evidence is one run per arm (one archive, 32 trials), so it is a lead, not a measured effect. The Phase 2 done criterion, taken literally (mean edge above 0 on `food-ahead` and `prey-ahead`, champions play the league without error), now holds on arm d, but weakly: 0.16 is half the memoryless reference's 0.31, and the predator still fails `bite-discrimination` at 0.00 in every arm, below random's 0.11. **Next lever: select on the bite.** The hunt world pays only for energy, so a brain that closes on prey (`approach-prey` 1.00 everywhere) and bites anything or nothing is not told apart from one that bites bitable prey. Candidates, in order: a hunt world with a mix of bitable and unbitable (too-big) prey so a blind biter loses energy to counter-bites or wasted bites; a second `hunt` stage with weakened or slow prey as in [hunter-practice.md](hunter-practice.md) (graded difficulty, as meerkat helpers do); repeating arm d over three archive seeds to see whether 0.16 holds. None changes the reward; each changes the world, as the Decisions table requires.

### Phase 4 - Train the bite (findings from T12 iteration 2)

**Targets:** none - champions stay local; the detectors are the `prey-ahead` and `prey-too-big` drills and the `bite-discrimination` reflex case, read per the record below

The record of "From outcome to task" for iteration 2.

**Observation.** In all four arms (a to d) the predator passes `approach-prey` (1.00; 0.56 in c) and fails `bite-discrimination` at 0.00, under the random brains' 0.11. `prey-ahead` is 0.00 in a to c and 0.16 (5 of 32 trials, +0.16) in d, against the memoryless reference's 0.31. `prey-too-big` is 1.00 everywhere, but its prey is bitable (a forager's size is capped by its store, sim-notes.md), so that win is for not biting and a predator that never bites also wins it.

**Theory.** The predator closes on prey and the bite step is not selected: the hunt world pays energy for a landed bite and nothing separates a brain that bites a bitable prey from one that bites anything, or never bites. `bite-discrimination` is a conjunction (bite the bitable prey and refrain from the too-big and the kin victim in one trial), so 0.00 fits three brains: never bites, always bites, or bites by chance. Nature: predators test and choose prey by condition and size, and a failed attack costs them (a kick, an injury, a wasted rush); lions and wolves take the young, old and weak, and hunters of many kinds learn to abandon prey they cannot take ([hunter-practice.md](hunter-practice.md), nature model). The world already prices a refused bite: `recoil` in `js/sim/feeding.js` charges `recoilEnergy` (20) per tick to a hunter that bites what `couldBite` refuses, and the ray classes show the brain the difference (`agentChannel` in `js/sim/ray-march.js`: `CH_PREY` for what `couldBite`, `CH_OTHER` otherwise). In the hunt worlds almost nothing is refused, because `huntSizeRatio` 0.8 lets a hunter of size 31 bite any victim up to 38.75 while a forager stops at size 33 (40.5 only with the size gene at its top, sim-notes.md), so the price is never charged and the sense is never needed.
