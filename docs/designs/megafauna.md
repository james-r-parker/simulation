# Megafauna

**Status:** In progress - Phase 1 chunk 1 (walking, seasonal routes, grazing, trampling and rendering) built; chunk 2 (stampede, life cycle, detectors and deferred screens) remains.
**Validated:** a188a3f (2026-11-01)
**Priority:** 3 - built after flora (Priority 2), which it extends: it reads flora's patch weights, per-item lifetimes, fertile-zone fungus and seeding path (user, 2026-10-30)
**Depends on:** [flora.md](flora.md), [natural-selection.md](natural-selection.md), [worker-and-renderer.md](worker-and-renderer.md)

Large moving bodies return to the world as herds of megafauna: slow animals that move in loose groups between food patches, flee hunters, graze, age, breed and die, and later leave dung and carry seeds. A blob cannot bite them and they have no brains. A blob that does not track where a stampede is heading is trampled, a blob that eats where a herd has been finds the food its dung grows, and a dead animal is a feast.

The movement skill is the point. A pooled ray gives position and not velocity, so a blob predicts where a body will be from two looks, which is what the memory cells are for. Dodging a stampede is a few ticks, following a herd is seasons ([principles.md](../principles.md), "Time has more than one speed").

The scenes it serves ([principles.md](../principles.md)): **Scavenge** (a herd carcass is the largest meal in the world), **Season changes the map** (herds follow the fruiting weights, so their routes turn with the year), **Cross or stay** (herds file through the wall gaps, carry seeds across and recolonise a basin that has no herd), **Refuge** (the rock rings are too tight for a herd animal), **A real chase** (a hunter near a herd starts a stampede that cuts across both of them), **Boom and bust** (a herd strips a patch, moves on, and the patch regrows), **Other blobs are the climate**, and **Something unnamed**. It serves the selection rule "Luck is not skill": contact is avoidable, and `eval.dodge_skill` (new) reads the dodge. It adds no fitness term: a trampled blob loses energy and may die, a blob that eats dung-grown food or a herd carcass is fed, and nothing else reaches selection ([principles.md](../principles.md), "Measure scenes, reward them almost never").

Scope: the herd model in `js/sim/`, contact damage and its death cause, the ninth pooled ray channel and its instinct, grazing, dung and seed carrying, the detectors, and the look in `js/app/renderer/`. Out of scope: a herd animal with a brain, a genome or any evolution; a blob bite on a herd animal; trampling of food; predators that hunt herds as prey; a second herd species.

Symbols marked (new) do not exist before this doc's task that adds them. Flora symbols come from [flora.md](flora.md) and are named with their flora task. Renderer symbols ([`LOOK_ITEMS` and `lookFor`](../../js/app/renderer/quality.js), [`SCENERY_MAX_LUMINANCE`](../../js/app/renderer/colors.js), the [rock shape](../../js/app/renderer/rock-shape.js)) are in the tree: the Look PR (`e2cd534`) is merged into the big-bang branch.

## The herds

A herd is a group of animals. The world holds `herdCount` herds (0 turns the feature off) split over the two basins in alternation. An animal is a circle of radius `herdRadius` with a velocity, an age, a life span, an energy store, a group and a stable `herdId` (new; a world counter `herdNextId` assigned in slot order at creation and birth, hashed). Capacity is `herdMax` (new) = `herdCount x herdSize` slots.

### Stampede

Every 8th tick, staggered by group index, `senseHunters` (new, `js/sim/herd-startle.js`) loops the alive agents whose specialisation `canHunt` (`specParams` in `../../js/sim/spec-params.js`) and starts a stampede on any group that has an animal within `herdStartleRadius` of one: `groupAlarm` (new) becomes `herdStampedeTicks` and the group's flee vector is the unit vector from the nearest hunter to its centroid. While `groupAlarm` is above 0 the desired speed is `herdRunSpeed`, the heading is the flee vector bent by the repulsion terms, and the group does not graze. The alarm ticks down, and a hunter still in range starts it again. `HerdStampede` logs each start.

A walking herd moves at `herdWalkSpeed` 1.5 and a stampede at `herdRunSpeed` 6.5, a little below a blob's `maxVelocity` 8. An animal at 6.5 closes a 300 px sight in under 50 ticks, and a blob that sees it late and in a straight line cannot clear a lane 160 px wide. A blob that moves across the heading, or into a refuge, can. A hunter near a herd drives a stampede over its prey, and over itself.

### Contact

`trampleHerds(world, slot)` (new, `js/sim/trample.js`) runs in `integrate` right after `moveAgent` and before `wallAxis` and `bounceObstacles`, so a blob pushed out of an animal into a rock or out of the world is resolved the same tick by the existing wall clamp and rock bounce (which charge `wallDamage` and `obstacleDamage` as `COLLISION`: a blob pinned between a herd and a rock pays both). For every alive animal whose circle overlaps the blob's: the blob is pushed out along the centre normal, `vn = (v_blob - v_animal) . n` is the relative normal speed, and when `vn < 0` the blob's velocity is reflected by `v -= 2 vn n` and it loses

`trampleDamage x (closing / maxVelocity)^2 x (size / baseSize)`, where `closing = -vn`,

as `LOSS_CAUSE.TRAMPLE` (new). A walking herd (closing 1.5) costs 14 energy at the default 400, a stampede head on (closing 6.5 at size `baseSize`) 264, and the same speed against a fat blob of size 40 costs 420. A blob running away at the herd's speed has a closing speed near 0 and takes nothing. A blob pinned against a rock is pushed every tick and takes the damage every tick. A hunter feeding on a carcass is as exposed as any blob: the size term grows with its gorge store (`maxEnergyMultiplier` 3), so a stampede over a carcass kills a fat hunter. The stampede is not removed or weakened to raise `eval.predator_presence`. `deathCause` returns `DEATH_CAUSE.TRAMPLED` (new, 5 after flora's `POISONED`) when the last loss cause of an energy death is `TRAMPLE`; `AgentDied` carries the animal's `herdId` in the field that holds the killer's genome id for `EATEN`.

### Energy, life and death

Herd energy is world-held, like a food item: it is not in the ledger until a blob eats a carcass, which books it through `addEnergy` as food. Nothing reaches a blob from the herd except through `tryEat`, so the ledger invariant holds with no new source. Every joule of a herd's store comes from plant items it removed from the world unbooked, and a carcass returns a share of it to the world.

## The ninth sense

The herd body is cast against like a rock. Phase 1 writes it into the obstacle channel (`CH_OBSTACLE` in `../../js/sim/ray-march.js`, via a new `HERD` hit kind ranked after `SMOKE` in `../../js/sim/ray-gather.js`, listed by `appendFixtures`), so a blob sees a large moving rock and can already dodge it, with no layout change and no archive break. Phase 2 gives it its own channel.

- **Channel.** `RAY_CHANNELS` goes from 9 to 10 with `CH_MEGAFAUNA` (new) 9, the flag appended so no earlier offset moves. `POOLED_CHANNELS` gets a ninth name, `megafauna`, after `smoke`: the ray part of the pooled row is the nine channels of the [Sensory schema](brain-capacity-and-memory.md). A herd hit writes the flag 1 and the proximity as every non-agent hit does; `poolRays` pools it with the unchanged code. It is not a threat, prey, kin or other, so no existing channel changes meaning, and the animal blocks rays behind it as a rock does.
- **Layout.** The layout is the megafauna step of the [Sensory schema](brain-capacity-and-memory.md) (89 inputs). `rawInputCount` is `rayCount x 10 + 33`; `inputOffsets` and every `wSkip` row follow the schema.
- **Genome.** The genome sizes are the megafauna row of the [Sensory schema](brain-capacity-and-memory.md), and `ARCHIVE_VERSION` in `../../js/evo/archive-codec.js` goes from 5 to 6 (an archive or champion of another length or version is refused, naming the field).

### Instinct

`applyInstinct` adds a wary reflex to every body, hunters included: thrust on the strength of the strongest megafauna hit and a turn away on its sine, each at `herdWary` x `weight` (`herdWary` 0.5, half the strength of the threat reflex). No reflex approaches a herd: following one, which pays only through dung food and carcasses, has to evolve against the wary reflex. The terms are added after the random fill with no draws, so `instinctWeight` 0 and `herdWary` 0 leave a genome bit-identical.

## The look

Phase 1 draws the animals as plain discs in a `createHerdLayer` (new) that reuses the rock shaders, so the herds can be watched. Phase 4 replaces it. ## Phases

### Delivery

Megafauna is built after flora is `Implemented`, in four phases, each on its own branch from the latest `origin/main`. Phase 1 has two chunks (AGENTS.md, "Build in chunks"), each its own branch and PR; the other phases have one. A task that moves `worldHash` pins the short goldens in its own commit; each chunk re-pins the long goldens once at its end (Node, then `npm run browser:hash` in Chromium). Every `npm run bench`, `bench-compare`, `eval`, `eval:power` and `screen` run is in a Runs row, never in a code commit. After the runs, an Adopt task sets the defaults to the best arm in a commit of its own that pins the goldens it moves. The conformance review closes each phase and runs in parallel with the final `scripts/check.sh`. A numeric value is a config key; a boolean with one chosen value is not a key.

## Feature evaluation

Every phase is evaluated by the same five steps, in its Runs tasks, and passes only if the whole ecosystem holds and not only its own mechanics:

1. **Baseline.** A run with the feature absent on the same seeds (`E75` for Phase 1 and the previous phase's final defaults after it).
2. **Controlled test.** A staged hand-built scene with a known answer for the new interaction (a hunter near a herd starting a stampede, a blob in a lane at a known closing speed, a herd animal dying beside a blob, a carcass with two feeders).
3. **Existing populations.** The effect per specialisation and per family per basin, and the predator gate (`eval.predators_at_end`, `eval.prey_types_at_end`, `eval.basin_lineage_effective`), against the baseline.
4. **16-seed compare.** `npm run eval:compare` of the feature enabled against the baseline.
5. **Bench at the maximum.** `npm run bench` at the intended maximum population (`maxAgents` 500 and `herdMax` animals alive), under the CPU lock.
