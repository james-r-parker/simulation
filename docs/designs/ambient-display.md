## The director on air

TV mode is the unattended state. It starts with `?tv=1`, with the `T` key, and after 5 minutes without input (`createIdleTracker` in `js/app/idle.js`). Any input leaves it and restores the camera, the speed and the HUD.

- In TV mode the director (`createDirector`) owns the camera: wide, glide, linger and pullback shots chosen from the moment scores of `js/app/director/scoring.js`. [worker-and-renderer.md](worker-and-renderer.md) T30 (the wiring, the caption, the TV border), T52 (`director/pace.js`), T51 (the era card) and T50 (`npm run director:dry`) are built as this doc's first chunk and marked built there.
- A linger shot on one agent holds that moment with the director's own spring (`applyShotToCamera` in `director/director.js`); the follow of this doc ("Follow as a story") does not step while the director holds the camera (`js/app/frame-loop.js`), so the two never fight over it.
- The schedule has a floor and a ceiling: no shot under 30 s (6 s until Phase 7), no shot over 90 s, and a wide shot at least every 10 minutes.
- A quiet world is paced: `director/pace.js` raises the sim speed while no moment scores above its threshold and restores x1 when one does, so a kill is never watched at x4.
- Variety: the director keeps the last 20 shot kinds and subjects and scores a repeat down (`director.max_kind_share` in `director:dry`).

## A death that reads

A death has three beats, all on the wall clock, all anchored to the body.

| Beat     | Length                  | What is drawn                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Failing  | 0 to 400 ms             | The live loops keep running: `packBody` no longer zeroes the motion and body lanes of a pending body. The swim loop slows to rest over the beat. There is no frozen glide: a pending body with no record after 400 ms starts the starvation clip                                                                                                       |
| Breaking | 500 to 1400 ms by cause | The membrane fails. The membrane SDF gains a `tear` parameter (0 to 1): angular gaps open from the bite bearing for `EATEN`, from the lowest point for `STARVED`, and at scattered bearings for `DISEASE` and `POISONED`. The rim colour drains toward the palette's `rot` brown before any opacity falls. Organelle parts drift outward a body radius |
| Leaving  | 400 to 800 ms           | What is left fades. For `EATEN` the carcass model fades in over this beat as the body reaches `CARCASS_SCALE`, so two outlines never overlap at full strength                                                                                                                                                                                          |

- Particles spawn at the body's drawn position (`positionOf` in `js/app/renderer/dying.js`) and scale with the body's size, on the wall clock, so they end with the clip at any sim speed.
- Each of the seven causes in `DEATH_ANIMS` has its own breaking beat: `EATEN` (torn from the bite), `STARVED` (deflate, then split), `DISEASE` (pitting), `POISONED` (the fill clouds, then pits), `OLD_AGE` (a slow even fade with no tear), `COLLISION` (a flat split along the impact normal), `TRAMPLED` (flattened along the herd's heading, then split).
- A starving body shows it before it dies: under 15% energy the swim loop amplitude and the fill fall together (a `failing` condition loop in `js/app/renderer/clips.js`), so a death by starvation is anticipated.
- Birth: `birthPop` loses its elastic overshoot. A split is a fission (the parent's outline pinches and the child separates along the pinch axis); a mating birth is a bud that swells to size on an ease-out with no bounce.

The organelle drift of the breaking beat is open as T10b: the rig moves the whole model, not parts, so until a per-part channel exists the whole model tears.

The `tear` value rides the existing fade lane of a dying row, so the 16-attribute limit is not approached (10 used).

## Follow as a story

- **Hold through the death.** `stepFollow` holds for the death clip's length plus 600 ms, then hands off.
- **Hand off with a reason.** The next subject is, in order: the killer (when the death was `EATEN`), a living child, the nearest living kin of the same lineage, then `pickFollowAgent`. A caption names the relation: "its killer", "its daughter", "its kin". It stays up while the calm move to the new subject runs and for 3 s after the move ends.
- **Hand off calmly.** A handoff the follow makes on its own (after a death, or from boredom) is the calm move of "A calm camera" (`js/app/follow-move.js`): zoom out, pan, zoom in, on the wall clock, aimed each frame at where the follow aims now, and the spring takes over with no error. A viewer's own pick keeps the spring.
- **Framing.** `followSubject` aims ahead of the body by its velocity times 0.4 s, capped at a fifth of the short side. During a pursuit (`js/app/pursuit.js`) it frames both bodies: the target is their midpoint and the zoom keeps both inside the middle 60% of the view.
- **Zoom breathes.** Zoom eases toward a target set by state: closer when the subject eats, mates or gives birth, wider with speed and in a pursuit. Manual zoom overrides it until the next handoff.
- **Boredom.** After 120 s with one subject and no event scoring above the movement score, follow hands off to `pickFollowAgent`'s best other subject. Manual selection disables the timeout for that subject.
- **Subject plate.** A small plate beside the ring: family colour and name, specialisation, age in seasons, children alive, kills, and the current condition (starving, pregnant, cold, hunted). It fades after 6 s and returns on a change; it takes the pixel shift of `applyPixelShift`. The ring becomes a smooth SDF ring.
- **Life line.** With the inspector open on a followed subject, the inspector follows the handoff and shows the subject's last 12 events as a strip (born, meal, bite given, bite taken, mate, birth, kill).

## The scene reel

A behaviour change is reviewed by watching it. The reel is the clip of each scene, cut by the app, not chosen by hand.

- While the app runs, `js/app/reel.js` keeps a ring (`reel-ring.js`) of the last 12 s of the camera and subject, and on a moment of a scene kind (`js/app/scene-counters.js`: a new instance of a scene with a signal) cuts a 10 s clip: 6 s before the moment, 4 s after. A moment's score is the number of instances of its scene in the last 10 s of ticks; a scene starts a clip only when the score beats its best, at most once per 5 s of ticks, one clip at a time, so the reel keeps the best-scored clip per scene per world. The clip comes from a rolling recorder (`js/app/reel-recorder.js`: one `MediaRecorder` on the canvas for as long as the world runs, a chunk a second, the header chunk and the last 12 s kept), not from `js/app/capture.js`, whose recorder starts on a press and cannot reach back 6 s; the encoder runs off the frame loop and the frame only reads the clock. With `?reel=1` (the script's switch) a moment also follows its subject, as the director's follow would.
- The insights drawer gains a Reel tab (the last tab, so the others keep their indices): one tile per scene, the clip or `not seen yet`, the tick it happened and a button to jump the world's follow to a live instance.
- `npm run reel -- --seed <n> --ticks <t> [--config <file.json>] [--policy <name>] --out <dir>` runs the app headless in Chromium (the browser smoke's session, `scripts/lib/browser-session.mjs`) at x8 and writes one clip per scene seen and `reel.json` (scene, tick, score, file). The page reads the config file through the `config` URL parameter (base64url JSON of numbers and booleans, a base the `agents`, `hold` and `merit` parameters override) and hands the clips to the script through `window.__reel`, which is installed in every build because the smoke runs the production build. On the WebGL2 software path the look differs from the real GPU; the reel shows behaviour, not the look.
- A PR that changes sim, evo or brain behaviour attaches the reel of seed 1 before and after for the scene it names (`create-pr` skill). The reel is a detector's companion, never fitness.
- The weekly watch: once a week the user watches the reel of the default world and the champions, and what the user says becomes tasks in the plan that owns the scene (`docs/principles.md`, "How the principles are used").

## A calm camera

> "that ui feature make me sea sick, its just jumping around the screen zoomed in sickness for realz, it needs to jump alot less and probably and a nice zoom out pan and zoom in when a change needs to happen" - the user, 2026-11-21

What the director and the follow do today, from `js/app/director/shots.js`, `schedule.js`, `pace.js` and `js/app/camera.js`:

- A `glide` is a straight smootherstep in position and zoom over 2 s (kills) or 3 s, from a wide pose (the whole 14400 x 8100 world on a 3840 wide canvas, zoom 0.27) to a close frame 1600 world units across (zoom 2.4, 9 times closer). That is up to 9 times the zoom and up to 70% of the screen of travel in 2 s to 3 s, at a peak of several screens a second at the close end.
- `momentFrameWidth` is the closest the director goes (1600, 2400 or 3600 units across). `ZOOM_IN` (24) is only the manual limit, `maxZoom = homeZoom x ZOOM_IN`; the director never reaches it.
- The cadence: a `wide` shot lasts 20 to 40 s, a `linger` 8 s (extended by 4 s steps to 20 s), a `pullback` 4 s, the floor is 6 s, and decisions run every 500 ms. A busy world cuts about every 30 s: 117 to 142 shots an hour in the readings above.
- A new `wide` shot snaps to its new framing (`shotPose` returns `toPose` for `wide`, with no easing from the last pose), and a `linger` follows the moment with a first-order spring that starts at full speed, so each arrival lurches.
- A follow handoff (`switchTo` in `js/app/follow.js`) moves the spring's target to the new subject at once; the camera leaves at `POSITION_RATE` (3.08) times the distance a second, which is several screens a second for a far subject.

The rule: **every change of subject is one eased move, and there are fewer of them.**

- **The move** (`js/app/camera-move.js`, pure): zoom out until the current and the next subject both sit inside the middle 70% of the view (never closer than the current zoom, never wider than the whole world), pan at that zoom, zoom in to the next shot's zoom. Each leg is a smootherstep, so the speed and the zoom rate are zero at every joint: no velocity step. Zoom is eased in its logarithm, so the zoom factor a second is steady.
- **The caps**, from comfort guidance (a view that moves faster than about a third of the screen a second, or changes scale faster than about 1.5 times a second, reads as lurching on a screen that fills the field of view): `MAX_PAN_SCREENS_PER_S` 1/3 of the canvas width a second at the peak of the ease, `MAX_ZOOM_FACTOR_PER_S` 1.5 at the peak of the ease. A leg takes as long as its cap needs, so a far or deep move is long and a near one short.
- **Already on screen:** when the next subject is inside the middle 70% at the current zoom and the next zoom is not wider, the zoom out is skipped and the pan runs at `SLOW_PAN_SCREENS_PER_S` 1/6.
- **Where it applies:** the director's `glide` (the move to the moment), `pullback` (the move back to a wide framing) and every new `wide` framing, and a follow handoff (a death's killer, child or kin, and boredom). A viewer's own pick or `F` press keeps the spring: it answers an input. A hard cut never happens; a fast glide at close zoom cannot, since the pan cap is in screen terms and the move zooms out first.
- **The tone at arrival:** a `linger` ramps its follow spring up over `LINGER_EASE_IN_S` (3 s), so the camera does not lurch to a moment that moved while the glide ran.
- **Fewer cuts:** `WIDE_MIN_S` 30 (was 8), `WIDE_BASE_S` 30 and `WIDE_SPAN_S` 30 (a wide of 30 to 60 s plus its move, was 20 to 40), `LINGER_BASE_S` 16 (was 8), `LINGER_MAX_S` 40 (was 20), `SHOT_FLOOR_S` 30 (was 6) and the new `SHOT_HOLD_MIN_S` 30, which `lingerOver` and `decideWide` both enforce: a shot, its glide and linger, runs at least 30 s before it pulls back, and no subject is cut to within 30 s of the last cut. The shortest cycle (30 s shot, a pullback of about 6 s, a 30 s wide) is about 66 s: at most about 55 shots an hour. `director:dry` checks both readings.
- **Pace:** a wide shot at x4 would run its move at four times the cap, so `director.js` asks `pace.js` for x1 while any move is in flight and for the quiet speed only once the camera has settled.
- **Less tight:** the closest frames widen from 1600, 2400 and 3600 world units across to 3600, 4800 and 6000: at most 4 times `homeZoom` (was 9), 1.07 pixels a world unit on a 4K canvas (was 2.4). A blob is still a few tens of pixels on the 4K OLED, and the viewer who wants closer presses `F` or zooms.

### Phase 8 - Playable scene clips

**Targets:** browser.frame_p95_ms, browser.draw_cpu_p95_ms

A cut belongs to one world and starts at a decodable frame. The rolling recorder starts a new recording when the world changes and ignores chunks delivered by the old recorder after reset. The Reel tab shows when the new world is collecting its first pre-roll. A clip requires recorded frames on both sides of the scene moment. The first six seconds of a new recording cannot supply that pre-roll.

A Chromium browser fixture writes a known scene moment over visibly different old and new worlds. It requires a clip, opens the exported file in a video element and checks decoded frames before and after the moment. Phase 6's frame budgets also hold with a full rolling buffer.

A saved reel carries the seed, policy, accepted config overrides and worker `rulesHash` in `reel.json`. A published-champion reel also identifies its loaded champion set by content hash. The manifest supplies the inputs to rerun the reel and compare its rules and champion set before treating it as the same world.

## Decisions

| Decision                                                                               | Holds because                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The membrane tears; nothing explodes                                                   | The art direction is restrained; a cell lyses, it does not burst into sparks                                                                                           |
| Light is added on events only                                                          | [crisp-renderer.md](crisp-renderer.md) forbids idle glow; a 500 ms falloff on a kill keeps that rule and reads at distance                                             |
| The variety penalty ages out after 15 simulated minutes                                | The repeat penalty is the kind's share of the last 20 shots; without an age cutoff a world with one kind of moment is shut out for good                                |
| The insights drawer computes nothing in the sim                                        | Detectors are read, never rewarded (`docs/principles.md`); the drawer reads posted stats and the app's own logs                                                        |
| A fission pinches the newborn, not the parent                                          | The BORN record carries only the child's genome                                                                                                                        |
| Every look change is verified on real WebGPU                                           | Software GL does not show the rim or MSAA as the OLED does                                                                                                             |
| Every change of subject is zoom out, pan, zoom in                                      | The user got sea sick from jumps at close zoom (2026-11-21); a pan at a wide zoom is slow on screen whatever the world distance, and each leg of the move ends at rest |
| The caps are 1/3 screen width a second and 1.5 times zoom a second, at the ease's peak | Comfort guidance for a view that fills the field of view; a cap on the peak is a cap on every frame, and the leg lengthens to meet it                                  |
| A subject already on screen gets a slow pan at 1/6                                     | There is nothing to zoom out for, and a calm look is slower than the cap                                                                                               |
| Shots are 20 to 60 an hour, held at least 30 s                                         | The user wants far fewer jumps; a 66 s shortest cycle gives at most about 55 an hour and a quiet world gives fewer                                                     |
| The bar `director.shots_per_hour` moves from 60 to 240 to 20 to 60                     | The user's direction, not a weakening: the old bar named the cadence that made the user sick                                                                           |
| The closest frame is 3600 units across (4 times home), not 1600 (9 times)              | A blob is still tens of pixels on 4K; the viewer who wants closer presses `F`                                                                                          |
| A handoff caption outlasts the calm move by 3 s                                        | A far move takes over 4 s; a 3 s caption from the handoff was gone before the camera arrived (T17)                                                                     |
| A viewer's own pick keeps the spring                                                   | An input answers at once; only the unattended moves are eased                                                                                                          |
| The move runs at x1                                                                    | A move at x4 would run at four times its cap; `pace.js` gives x4 back once the camera settles                                                                          |
