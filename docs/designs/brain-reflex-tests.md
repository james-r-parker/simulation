# Brain reflex tests

**Status:** In progress - Phase 1 built: the six cases, the table builder, the batched evaluator, the bars, `npm run reflex` and `npm run bench:reflex` (522 genomes per second, 776 times the drills); Phase 2 has its study script (T9) but `E-RT-1` has not run, so the tier has no verdict and no use yet.
**Validated:** d6ca3e6c (2026-11-07)
**Priority:** 25 - a tool and a study that change no sim behaviour; it builds on the trainer and drills (training.md) and runs on the Rust brain kernel (wasm-kernels.md, adopted), so it queues behind both
**Depends on:** [training.md](training.md)

Idea (user, 2026-11-07): train or test a brain on its own, with known inputs and known good outputs - danger in sight: run; food to one side: turn towards it; do not crash. A brain that passes these fast checks is then trained in a sim.

A reflex case is a short run of input rows fed to one brain with nothing around it: no world step, no physics, no other agent. The rows are built by the sim's own sensing code, so a pass in a case means the brain gives that output to that input row in the world. Reflex cases are the third tier of three:

| Tier         | Judges                                   | Cost per genome                     | Lives in                      |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Reflex cases | The brain's outputs on staged input rows | About 10^3 brain forwards, no world | `js/evo/reflex-*.js` (new)    |
| Drills       | A subject's behaviour in a 3-agent arena | About 10^5 world ticks              | `js/evo/drill.js`             |
| The sim      | Survival and offspring in a world        | A whole run                         | `js/sim/`, `js/evo/policies/` |

A reflex score is a detector and a filter. It never enters fitness, merit, mate rank, archive rank or any live-world rule ([principles.md](../principles.md), Measure scenes, reward them almost never). Drills and the sim stay the judge.

## Reflex cases

`js/evo/reflex-cases.js` (new) exports `REFLEX_CASES`, a frozen list in the shape of `DRILLS` in `js/evo/drill-scenarios.js`: each case has a `name`, the `scene` it serves, the subject `spec` (forager 0, predator 1), `plan(rng, view)` returning the staged items for one trial (a `Stage` as `stageWorld` in `js/evo/pool-discrimination-stage.js` takes, one per tick of the sequence), and `check(outputs, staging)` returning whether one tick's outputs pass (a `Staging` carries `kind`, `bearing` and `mirror`). `view` is the subject's ray length from `viewOf` in `js/evo/memory-probe-run.js`; placement helpers `heading` and `around` and the arena constants come from `js/evo/memory-probe-scenarios.js`.

### Input construction

Input rows are never typed. `buildCaseTable` in `js/evo/reflex-table.js` (new) stages each trial's tick with `stageWorld` (the arena knobs of `arenaConfig` in `js/evo/memory-probe-run.js`: no refuges, no hazards, rules restored), builds the hash with `buildHash` from `js/sim/spatial-hash.js` as `step` does, runs `senseAll` from [sensors.js](../../js/sim/sensors.js) once, and copies the subject's raw `inputs` row (`rayCount x RAY_CHANNELS + EXTRA_INPUTS` values) and its `rayHue` row (`rayCount` values, which pooling turns into the two food hue inputs) into `Float32Array` tables, one row per sequence and tick. A staging whose items do not move senses one tick and repeats the row. Rays, the body block, the scent block and the refuge inputs are therefore what `castRays`, `writeBodyInputs` and `writeScentInputs` produce for that scene, and a change to the sensing code or the layout changes the table, not a hand-kept index. The pooled row is made at evaluation time by `poolRays` inside `forwardAll`, as in the world. A case that must set a body input the stage cannot (age, pregnancy) reads its index through `BODY_INPUT` and `inputOffsets` in `js/sim/brain-layout.js`, never a literal. The table is built once per layout, config and seed and is shared by every genome; it is the row of a body with vision gene 0 (the `viewOf` default for null weights), since `visionOf` in `js/sim/traits.js` scales sight by the genome's vision gene; it is stamped with `BRAIN_LAYOUT_NAME` and the `rulesHash` of its config, and `ReflexTableStale` is thrown when either differs at evaluation.

- A sequence is `SEQUENCE_TICKS` (6) rows. Hidden state, Hebbian `fastOut` (present when `hebbianRate` is above 0, the arena's default) and the memory read-back inputs start from reset for every trial: `hidden` 0, `fastOut` copied from the genome's `wOut` block, `MEMORY_A` and `MEMORY_B` read 0 on tick 1 and then the slot's own last outputs, written at `inputOffsets(config).rawBodyStart + BODY_INPUT.MEMORY_A` and `+ BODY_INPUT.MEMORY_B` as `writeBodyInputs` does.
- Ticks 1 to `SETTLE_TICKS` (2) are not scored: a recurrent brain may need a tick or two to commit. A trial passes when `check` holds on every tick from 3 to 6 of every staging it holds (a trial is one draw of the case's stream: its stagings and their mirror images). The tick-1 pass share is reported beside the score as `fast`, and is not part of it.
- A moving item (a threat closing) is re-staged along its path each tick, so the rows change as they would in a chase.
- Outputs are read as the sim reads them: `OUTPUT_INDEX.TURN` and `OUTPUT_INDEX.THRUST` in [-1, 1] (`actAll` in `js/sim/physics.js` turns by `TURN x maxTurn x turnBySpec`), `SPRINT` and `BITE` above 0 as the actions fire.

### Cases

Bearing `b` is the item's angle in the subject's frame, positive towards positive `TURN`: a turn of sign `b` rotates the heading towards the item (`actAll` adds `TURN x maxTurn x turnBySpec` to the heading). Turn thresholds are shares of the output range; the numbers are starting values that T4 calibrates against the reference controller and the baselines below, and the calibrated values replace them in this table.

| --------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `flee-threat`         | A real chase                          | forager  | A hunter (a predator, `couldBite` the subject) closing from 0.5 to 0.3 of view, `abs(b)` 20 to 180 degrees. A ray senses only a target wider than the 22.5 degrees between two rays, so the path is the near half of the view (0.7 to 0.4 left the hunter unseen on a tenth of the scored ticks)                                                                                                                                                  | thrust at least 0.3 and the turn is away from the threat (sign opposite to `b`, size at least 0.15); a threat dead behind (`abs(b)` over 160) passes on thrust at least 0.5                                                                                                 |
| `approach-food`       | Leave a dying patch                   | forager  | One food at 0.4 to 0.8 of view, `abs(b)` 15 to 160 degrees (dead behind sits on a ray whose turn side is a tie); 15 percent of trials put it within 5 degrees of the heading instead, the ray-0 cone                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | turn towards the food (sign of `b`, size at least 0.15) and thrust above 0.1; food within 5 degrees: thrust at least 0.3 and `abs(turn)` at most 0.5                                                                                                                        |

- Each negative staging is scored in the same trial as its positive control (`bite-discrimination`, `avoid-obstacle`), so a constant brain cannot pass half a case. Every trial is also run mirrored (`b` becomes `-b`, left becomes right) and passes only when both pass, so a constant turn scores 0 on the sign cases. `approach-food` is the positive control of `flee-threat`.
- Foragers face 4 cases and predators 2; a genome's **reflex score** is its mean pass share over the cases of its specialisation, as the drill edge skips a drill whose `spec` does not match (`skipReason` in `js/evo/drill.js`).
- Each case runs `REFLEX_TRIALS` (16) trials from `fork(createRng(seed), 'reflex:<name>')`; its pass share is passes over trials.
- **Mate approach** is left out: `reproduction.js` pairs a ready agent with a partner inside `mateRange` on the `MATE` output alone, so a brain that always outputs `MATE` above 0 passes any mate case and the case separates nothing. Mating stays judged by the sim.
- **Alarm, hunt call, smoke and split** are left out: the split output reads 0 (the split rule reads energy and age), and the call and smoke scenes are judged by `eval.alarm_rate` and their own detectors.

### Baselines and a valid case

Each case prints, as the drills do, three bars on its own trials:

- **Random brains.** `REFLEX_RANDOM_BRAINS` (64) rows from `randomWeights` in `js/evo/mutation.js` (stream `fork(createRng(seed), 'reflex-random:<name>:<i>')`); the case's baseline is their mean pass share, and a genome's **edge** is its score minus the mean over the same cases. Evaluation is cheap enough for a tight bar.
- **Constant brains.** All outputs 0; full thrust and no turn; full thrust and full left turn; `BITE` always above 0. The case's constant bar is the best of the four.
- **Reference controller.** `referenceSubject('memoryless')` from `js/evo/memory-probe-references.js`: its `act` reads `world.inputs` and writes `world.outputs`, so the reflex evaluator runs it on the same rows in place of `forwardAll`.

A case is valid when the reference passes at least 0.9 and both the random mean and the constant bar are at most 0.5. `pick-safe-food` uses `hueAwareSubject` from `js/evo/reflex-references.js` as its reference: the memoryless one seeks the nearest food and ignores hue, so it fails by design; the hue-aware one pools the rays and heads for the hit whose hue is nearer the safe plant's. The ahead set of `approach-food` is narrowed to within 5 degrees, the ray-0 cone, where `turnTowards` (which returns -1, 0 or 1 per ray) can pass it. A case that fails validity is rewritten before it lands; the table of bars goes in [sim-notes.md](sim-notes.md).

## Evaluation path

`js/evo/reflex-eval.js` (new) exports `createReflexWorld(config, slots)` and `evalReflex(genomes, tables, options)`.

- One world holds one slot per genome, built once per batch by `createReflexWorld` (`stageWorld` with one agent per genome; `loadGenome` writes a row and runs `applyGenes`). A batch is one specialisation, up to `BATCH_SLOTS` (512) genomes; the sequences of a case run one after another through it.
- Per sequence, per tick, `evalReflex` copies the table's row into every slot's `world.inputs` and `rayHue`, rewrites the two memory read-backs, calls `forwardAll` ([brain.js](../../js/sim/brain.js)) once for the whole batch and scores `world.outputs` with `check`. `senseAll` and every other phase are never called. Before every sequence the state is reset (`hidden`, `fastOut`, `outputs`). A reference controller runs alone on the same rows, in place of `forwardAll`. Options carry an `observe` hook and a `ReflexCase` debug log of each trial's first failing tick.
- `forwardAll` already runs every alive slot in one call, so the batch is across genomes with no new kernel. Every world runs the Rust kernel through that call ([wasm-kernels.md](wasm-kernels.md), T7: there is no `wasmKernels` switch and no JS brain), so there is one path to bench. A batched entry point that wasm-kernels.md adds later changes the call, not the cases.
- A batch result is independent of batch size and slot order: `--workers 1` and `--workers 2` print the same table (a test pins it), and a genome scores the same alone, among 32, in reverse order and in batches of 5.

Throughput target: `bench.reflex_genomes_per_s` (`npm run bench:reflex`, one core, a forager, the four forager cases of 16 trials: 12 sequences a trial (flee 2, approach-food 2, pick-safe-food 2, avoid-obstacle 6, each with its mirror), 6 ticks each, so 1152 brain forwards per genome) at least 300, and at least 50 times the genomes per second of `drillSubject` over the forager drills on the same kind of genomes. The first draft said 900 on 768 forwards, which its own arithmetic did not support (about 2.5 microseconds a forward gives about 500 on 768, and the real count is 1152); 300 is that arithmetic with a margin. Measured, 2026-11-07, under `flock /tmp/claude-1000/cpu.lock` with other streams' evals running: **522 genomes per second** (1.9 ms a genome, 1.66 microseconds a forward including the scoring), **0.67 for the drills** (2 genomes, 5 forager drills, 32 trials each), **776 times**. Both bars are met.

`npm run reflex -- --champions <dir> | --archive <file> | --policy <name>` (`scripts/reflex.mjs`, new, the sources of `npm run drill`; `--cases a,b`, `--seed <n>` (1), `--trials <n>` (16), `--config <file.json>`, `--json`, `--out <path>`, `--workers <n>`) prints one row per genome: pass share and edge per case, the score, the edge and `fast`, then the bars.

## Use

Reflex scores are used in two places and never in a third. Whatever the use, the live policy's fitness, merit, mate rank and archive rank read nothing from this module, and a test pins it (`test/evo/reflex-isolation.test.js`, built with Phase 1: no file under `js/sim/`, `js/evo/policies/`, `js/worker/` or `js/app/`, and no other `js/evo/` module, imports a `reflex-*` module).

### The gate (Phase 3)

A cheap triage of what the expensive tiers spend time on, never of what lives.

- **Checkpoint triage.** `npm run train -- --reflex-gate <edge>` (`scripts/lib/train-args.mjs`; the checkpoint code in `scripts/lib/train-checkpoint.mjs`) computes the reflex score of every champion at a checkpoint and runs the drills and the 4-seed eval only on those at or above the edge. The curve row gains `reflex` (the mean score over all champions) and `reflexPassed` (how many passed). The archive, the offers and the episodes are untouched, so archives with and without the flag are byte-identical.
- **Screen before drills.** `npm run drill` and `npm run league` take `--reflex-gate <edge>` and skip a genome under it, reporting it as `-` with the reason `reflex`.

### Founder pre-training (Phase 4)

A stage before the trainer's generation 0, judged by paired arms against random founders and kept only if it wins.

`npm run train:reflex -- --count <n> --stop-at <share> --out <archive.json>` (`scripts/reflex-pretrain.mjs`, new) evolves `n` independent lineages per specialisation, each from its own random brain (`randomWeights`), by truncation selection and `mutateWeights` on the reflex score alone, on case seeds disjoint from the validation seeds. Each lineage stops the first generation its reflex pass share reaches `--stop-at` (default 0.8), not at the maximum, and lineages never exchange genes, so the founders are `n` different brains that clear a low bar, not one brain tuned to the cases. The result is an archive: each founder is placed by `seedArchive` in `js/evo/archive.js` with `n` 0 in a cell spread round-robin over the specialisation's `CELLS`, so any live offer replaces it and a founder the world does not like is gone after its first tested challenger. `npm run train -- --founders <archive.json>` (new flag) starts generation 0 from it in place of random brains; `--resume` continues a run and keeps its stage check, `--founders` does not.

### Why a reflex score is never fitness

- A score that selects breeds brains that pass the cases, not animals ([principles.md](../principles.md), Measure scenes, reward them almost never; Selection rule 9: do not pay a behaviour the children cannot repeat). The cases are a designer's picture of good behaviour on 6 to 10 stagings; the world has thousands.
- In the live world the only selection is survival and offspring. A founder that passes every case and loses in the world is removed by the world; the pre-training stage ends before any world exists, so it grants no child in one.
- The cases are fixed and public; a lineage that evolves against them for long overfits them. The stop-at bar, the independent lineages and the hold-out check (the validation seeds are never used by the pre-trainer) bound that.
- Drills and the sim are the judge of a trained brain. A change that raises reflex scores and lowers a drill edge or `eval.food_energy_per_1k` is a loss.
- The 2026-10-17 rule keeps scripted behaviour out of the archive. Pre-trained founders are evolved, not scripted, but the cases encode expected behaviour, so the stage is an option compared against random founders, and random founders stay the default until it wins.

## Validation

Does reflex pass rate predict drill and sim success? The study runs in Phase 2 on genomes that already exist, before any use is built.

**Genomes.** Every champion in `evals/league.json`, every champion file under `.sim/train/` exports, every archive elite of the archives in `evals/baselines/`, and a sample of `--save-champions` outputs from eval runs on seeds 1 to 16, early-generation checkpoint genomes of training runs and 32 random brains, so scores have spread: at least 120 genomes, and at least 40 per specialisation. Genomes of one run or lineage are not independent, so the interval resamples runs and lineages, not genomes. Case seeds are 101 to 116, never used by the pre-trainer.

**Outcomes, three, from cheapest to the one that matters.**

1. The mean drill edge of the genome (`npm run drill`, seed 1, 32 trials), per pair: `approach-food` with `food-ahead` and `food-behind`, `flee-threat` with `hunter-closing`, `no-bite-too-big` with `prey-too-big`, `approach-prey` with `prey-ahead`.
2. The genome's archive lower bound under the live fitness (what selection actually gave it).
3. The champion's `eval.champion_elo` from the league ([evolution-evals.md](evolution-evals.md)).

**Statistic.** Spearman rank correlation per pair with a BCa interval from `js/evo/compare.js` (resampling genomes, not trials), and the partial correlation controlling for specialisation.

**Prediction (written before the run, `E-RT-1`).** Each case's pass share against its matching drill's edge: rho at least 0.4, interval excluding 0, on at least 3 of the 4 pairs. Reflex score against archive lower bound and Elo: rho at least 0.2, interval excluding 0. The ways the world could differ: reflex cases ignore the chain (detect, commit, pursue, kill, survive), so a prey that flees correctly in one tick may not live; the rows are those of a vision-gene-0 body at rest (velocity and turn-rate inputs 0 for all ticks); selection on single-tick outputs favours open-loop behaviour; and an arena has no rocks, season or crowd.

**Grouping and counts.** The interval resamples clusters: every elite of one archive file is one cluster, each league champion and each random brain its own, a champion directory one. For the four drill pairs the genomes are of one specialisation, so the partial rho equals rho; it matters for the two outcome rows. `runStudy` warns when the genomes fall short of 120, or 40 of a specialisation, and `formatStudy` prints "underpowered" for an interval that could not be computed, so a missing interval is not read as a "no". `--random` adds to the genome count and does not by itself reach it.

**Known issue with the third pair.** The `prey-too-big` drill is meant to be won by not biting a prey too big to bite, but its prey (energy `2 x HUNTER_ENERGY`, a forager whose store caps at 2000, so size 33 against a hunter of 31 and `huntSizeRatio` 0.8) is bitable: `couldBite` is true. The drill is therefore won by a hunter that does not bite a bitable prey, and its edge says less about the case than the pair assumes. The study reports the pair as it stands; if it fails, T10 reruns it against a corrected drill (the prey needs its size gene at the top, as `bite-discrimination` stages it) before the verdict, and the drill fix is a drills change outside this doc.

**Decision rule.**

| Outcome of `E-RT-1`                                                                                                  | Verdict                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Drill rho at least 0.4 with interval excluding 0 on 3 of 4 pairs, and fitness and Elo intervals exclude 0            | Build both uses: the gate (Phase 3) and the pre-training arm (Phase 4)                                                                                          |
| Drill rho at least 0.4 with interval excluding 0 on 3 of 4 pairs, fitness or Elo interval includes 0                 | Filter only: build the gate (Phase 3); no pre-training                                                                                                          |
| Fewer than 3 pairs with rho at least 0.4 and an interval excluding 0 (`verdictOf` in `scripts/reflex-correlate.mjs`) | Drop: delete the `js/evo/reflex-*.js` modules, `scripts/reflex*.mjs`, the bench and their tests; the study and its numbers stay in [sim-notes.md](sim-notes.md) |

Phase 4 has its own experiment, `E-RT-2`, because a correlation among existing genomes does not show that selecting on the score helps: training with reflex-pre-trained founders against random founders, 16 train seeds from `evals/train-seeds.json` per arm paired by seed, the curriculum of `evals/train-curriculum.json`, 40 generations. Prediction: pre-trained founders reach a mean edge of 0.15 on `rock-refuge` (a drill no case resembles) in at most half the generations of random founders, and the final-generation `eval.food_energy_per_1k` (4-seed checkpoint eval) is `better` or `no-difference` at a minimum effect of 0.05 from `eval:compare` on the 16 pairs. A speed pass with a `worse` or underpowered final value means the stage bred case-passers: the arm is deleted.
