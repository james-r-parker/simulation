On the world after E112 (`maxFood` 3600, short-lived toxic caps) hunters live on carcasses and chance bites and starve:
the median `eval.hunter_kills_per_life_p50` is 0 on all 20 arms of E113 and `eval.hunter_zero_kill_share` is 0.80 to 0.92.
This doc teaches hunters to kill the way nature does: a parent feeds its young while they learn, the young stay near it
and burn less, the motor pattern is present at birth, and practice refines it inside a life. Each mechanism is a
default-off switch, screened on its own and adopted or deleted. Phase 1 is built: the flip reflex and the hunter
territory were adopted (always on); provisioning, the juvenile dependency and the sated-bite brake were deleted (E123). Phase 2 (reward-gated learning) lost its screen (E124) and was deleted.

## Principle - survival is not competence

Keeping a hunter alive long enough to learn is not the same as giving it the ability to learn. Provisioning, a lower
burn and a kin pull all lengthen a juvenile's life whether or not the juvenile ever hunts better; a fed child that
shadows its parent survives on the parent's kills. Survival and energy metrics (`eval.hunter_zero_kill_share`,
`eval.hunter_juvenile_death_share`, `eval.predators_at_end`, `eval.population_mean`) are guards and signs of life, never
the verdict.

- **Every adoption shows hunting competence improved.** A verdict to adopt a switch needs the age-matched kill
  conversion (`eval.hunter_kill_conversion_by_age.1k-2k`) up with the `eval:compare` interval excluding 0, and the
  independent kill share (`eval.hunter_independent_kill_share`) not down. A switch that only moves survival or energy
  is deleted.
- **Rewarded learning also had to beat its controls.** Phase 2 would have adopted `rewardedHebb` only if it beat a shuffled-reward arm
  (the same rewards at unrelated times) on the age-matched kill conversion; beating the defaults alone proves nothing,
  because a trace can change the plastic weights without teaching a link from act to meal.
- **A hunter that wipes out its prey is not a better world** ([principles.md](../principles.md), "Other blobs are the
  climate"). Every screen and confirmation reads the ecological guards (prey extinctions, boom and bust, population
  volatility) beside the competence metrics.

## Context

### Nature model

| Behaviour in nature                                                                                                                                                    | Maps to                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wolf pups are fed meat by the pack, carried in the stomach and regurgitated, until they travel with the pack (Mech 1970); lion and tiger cubs eat at the mother's kill | Provisioning: a hunter hands its young a share of each meal                                                                                                            |
| A cheetah mother releases live, weakened gazelle fawns for her cubs to catch; a domestic cat mother brings live prey home (Caro 1994; Caro 1980)                       | The young practise on prey the parent has already brought down; the share is paid from the parent's own meal                                                           |
| Meerkat adults give pups disabled scorpions, then live ones, as the pups age (Thornton and McAuliffe 2006)                                                             | Provisioning runs for a bounded window (`juvenileTicks`), then stops                                                                                                   |
| Predator young stay with the mother for months to years (lion 2 years, cheetah 18 months, wolf 10 months) while their skill grows                                      | Juvenile dependency: a lower burn during the window, and a pull towards kin (heritable, not age-gated; adults follow kin too, a pack-like side effect that is watched) |
| The stalk, chase, pounce and bite sequence is innate and refined by practice; kittens raised without their mother pounce but kill less often (Caro 1980)               | The hunter reflex at birth (`applyInstinct`, and `applyHunterReflex` for a flip child); reward-gated plasticity of the hidden-to-output path (`hebbianStep`)           |
| Practice is reinforced by the meal, not by the attempt                                                                                                                 | A reward trace gates the Hebbian update; no fitness term pays for a kill                                                                                               |

### Selection

[principles.md](../principles.md) rule 7 credits "kin that were fed" to the feeder, and rule 4 forbids restocking a
role. Provisioning moves energy a hunter has already earned to its own young; nothing enters the world. "Measure
scenes, reward them almost never" refuses a fitness or merit term for kills: the doc adds detectors, never a reward.

### Competence detectors (Phase 1)

These do not depend on survival: each has the hunters that never kill, or never hunt, in its denominator.

An **encounter** is a chase as `trackApproaches` already defines it: it opens at the first closing sighting of prey by a
`canHunt` agent and resolves caught, escaped or given up. Eligibility is built in (a hunter, prey in view and closing).
The chase tracker is reused, not replaced; the new scene reads each resolved chase with the hunter's age at its opening
and the hunter's slot. A chase cut short by the hunter's death, or whose victim dies of another cause, is dropped, as
there. **Kill conversion** is caught over caught plus escaped plus given up. Run-wide it equals `eval.chase_catch_share`;
the new metrics split it by the hunter's age at the opening of the chase, so an arm is judged on hunters of the same age
and not on lifetime kills.

| Metric                                      | Direction | Reads                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `eval.hunter_kill_conversion_by_age.<band>` | higher    | Kill conversion of the chases that open in the age band: `under1k` (age below 1000), `1k-2k` (1000 to 1999) and `over2k`. `1k-2k` is the primary target; the other two are `watch`. Null when no chase opened in the band                                                                                         |
| `eval.hunter_chases_by_age.<band>`          | watch     | Resolved chases in the band, so a conversion rests on a count and `eval:power` can size it                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `eval.hunter_first_kill_km_median`          | lower     | Kaplan-Meier median age at first kill. Every hunter born after burn-in is an observation: the event is the first deliberate kill, and a hunter that dies without one, or is alive at the last tick without one, is censored at its age. Null with the reason `median not reached` while the curve stays above 0.5 |
| `eval.hunter_first_kill_rmst`               | lower     | Restricted mean of the same Kaplan-Meier time up to the horizon `2 x juvenileTicks`: the area under the no-kill-yet curve. Always defined when a hunter was born, so it is the cited number while the median is unreached                                                                                         |
| `eval.hunter_independent_kill_share`        | higher    | Share of deliberate kills made by an independent hunter: one with no living parent within `PARENT_RANGE` (120 world units, `js/sim/dependent-killer.js`). The `HunterKill` payload carries the bit                                                                                                                |
| `eval.juvenile_parent_distance`             | watch     | Mean distance, at the 1000-tick checkpoints after burn-in, from each living juvenile hunter to its nearest living parent                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `eval.juvenile_sibling_distance`            | watch     | The same juveniles' mean distance to their living siblings (same `parentA` and `parentB`); a juvenile counts only with a living parent and a living sibling                                                                                                                                                       |
| `eval.juvenile_parent_pull`                 | watch     | `eval.juvenile_sibling_distance` over `eval.juvenile_parent_distance`: above 1 the juveniles stay nearer their parent than their siblings, near 1 the kin pull is a pull towards any kin. Null without a juvenile that has both                                                                                   |

### Ecological guards (every screen and confirmation)

| Metric                                                                   | Direction | Reads                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `eval.prey_extinctions`                                                  | lower     | Count, at the 1000-tick checkpoints after burn-in, of prey specialisations (not `canHunt`) that go from at least one alive to none. The seed frequency is the share of seeds with a count above 0, read from the per-seed values        |
| `eval.boom_bust_count`                                                   | lower     | Count of boom-bust episodes at the same checkpoints: hunters reach at least twice their last trough, then prey fall to at most half their level at that trough, then hunters fall to at most half their peak, all inside 20 checkpoints |
| `eval.predator_share_cv`, `eval.basin_pop_cv`, `eval.pred_prey_lag_corr` | watch     | Existing: population volatility of the hunters and the basins, and the lag between the two populations                                                                                                                                  |

A guard fails when `eval:compare` shows an increase of at least 5% of the before mean with the interval excluding 0 for a
`lower` or `watch` metric of this table, or a seed frequency of prey extinction above that of the reference arm.

Mechanisms:

- **Hunter reflex on a flip** (adopted at T12, always on). `applyInstinct` splits its `canHunt` branch into
  `applyHunterReflex`. `childBrain` in [natural-birth.js](../../js/evo/policies/natural-birth.js) calls it (through
  `applyBirthReflex`) at `instinctWeight`, on the child's row before mutation, when the child's specialisation `canHunt`
  and its mother's does not (the trigger is the specialisations, not `birthSource`, so mating and split children are
  covered alike). The prey terms are added on top of the copied or crossed row and clamp at `WEIGHT_CLAMP`; the flee
  terms the row carries from a non-hunter mother stay and evolution sorts them out. The call draws nothing. In `E115` it
  was the one lever that taught killing (conversion 1k-2k 0.038 to 0.094), and the taught hunters then emptied the world.
- **Hunter territory** (adopted at T12, always on, `E121` to `E123`). With the flip reflex hunters multiplied eightfold
  and the prey still crashed (`E119`): the over-hunting is the hunters' numerical response. In nature predator density is
  held below what the prey could feed by territory and breeding suppression (in a wolf pack only one pair breeds; lions,
  big cats and raptors hold ranges against their own kind). A `canHunt` agent may not start a pregnancy (mating,
  `isReadyToMate`) or split (`readyBeforeGate`) while more than `territoryMax` other mature `canHunt` agents lie within
  `territoryRadius` (`territoryAllows` in `js/sim/territory.js`). The count is a scan over the alive slots, run last in
  each ready test so it runs only for an agent that is otherwise ready; it draws nothing and allocates nothing.
  Non-hunters are untouched. A hunter that cannot breed still hunts and lives, so the lever acts on births, not on kills.
  `E123` (floor on, 16 seeds): flip with territory against the defaults, conversion 1k-2k 0.033 to 0.088, first-kill RMST
  4879 to 3150, hunters alive at the end on 14 of 16 seeds (10 of 16), prey types and prey extinctions not worse.
- **Tried and deleted** (`E123`, 16 seeds, floor on; none held):
    - **Provisioning** (`provisionEnabled`, `E115`, `E116`): a hunter handed 30% of a stored meal to its young in range,
      paid from its own meal (`provisionYoung`, `provisionTotals`, the `Provisioned` event, `provisionedTick`). Deleted at T10.
      The dependent bit of the independent-kill detector now reads only the living-parent rule.
    - **Juvenile dependency** (`juvenileDependency`, `E115`, `E116`): a juvenile hunter paid 0.6 of its cost
      (`juvenileBurnOf`) and every hunter child got a floor on the `kin` cells (`kinPullWeight`). Deleted at T11.
    - **Sated hunters do not bite** (`satedBite`, `E119`, `E120`): a full or digesting hunter skipped the bite in `lunge`.
      It kept the competence but not the prey; territory replaced it. Deleted at T12.
- **Competence detectors** (Detectors, no switch). A new `js/evo/scene-competence.js` reads the chase tracker: `trackApproaches`
  calls a `noteChase(slot, outcome, openedTick)` hook in the scene when a chase resolves, and the scene bins the outcome by
  the hunter's age at the opening (`ageTicks` minus the ticks since `openedTick`), from burn-in on. It also keeps, per hunter
  life, the first-kill age or the censoring age for the Kaplan-Meier time (a `HunterKill` with count 1, a `HunterLedger`
  death, and the alive hunters at the last tick). The independent-kill tally reads the `dependent` bit of each
  `HunterKill`. The bit is computed
  in `noteLedgerKill` by `isDependentKiller` in `js/sim/dependent-killer.js`: a parent (found by matching `parentA` and
  `parentB` to a living `genomeId`, as `slotOfGenome` does) is alive within `PARENT_RANGE` (120). The bit adds a field to the `HunterKill` payload; no new event.
- **Ecological guards** (no switch). A new `js/evo/scene-guards.js` samples, at the 1000-tick checkpoints after burn-in, the
  alive count of each prey specialisation and the hunter count, and computes `eval.prey_extinctions` and
  `eval.boom_bust_count` with the thresholds named at the top of the module (twice, half, 20 checkpoints). The three
  existing volatility metrics are read as they are.
- **Kin attraction detector** (no switch). The `kin` channel covers parents, children and siblings.
  `js/evo/scene-kin-proximity.js` samples, at the 1000-tick checkpoints, each living juvenile hunter's distance to its
  nearest living parent and its mean distance to its living siblings, and reports `eval.juvenile_parent_pull`. A pull to
  the parent shows as a ratio well above 1; a ratio near 1 says the juveniles follow any kin. It is read, not gated, and
  stays after the kin pull was deleted (T11) as a watch on whether juveniles stay near a parent.
- Brain layout, `inputCount` and `ARCHIVE_VERSION` do not change.

### Phase 3 - The bite reflex says no (proposed, 2026-11-07)

**Targets:** eval.hunter_kills_per_life_p50, eval.hunter_zero_kill_share, eval.hunter_kill_rate_by_source.mate-hunter-parent, eval.hunter_kill_rate_by_source.split-hunter-parent, eval.prey_types_at_end, eval.predators_at_end

The record of "From outcome to task" ([run-evolution-loop](../../.claude/skills/run-evolution-loop/SKILL.md)) for the review of 2026-11-07. Phase 1 gave hunters a prey reflex; Phase 2 and E124 showed that reward-gated Hebbian learning does not teach a kill. This phase asks whether the bite reflex itself says no where it should, and whether the world ever makes it need to.

**Gate.** The phase starts after [training.md](training.md) T20 has written the lever line (Phase 4, "Diagnosis gate"). The review's reading: the brain never bites, it bites everything, or it bites right and the chase fails; only the second row opens (a) and (b). T26 reads the line and the baseline and writes which parts run.

**Detector check (step 0).** `bite-discrimination` is a conjunction, so its pass share hides which staging failed; the three staging shares of training.md T18 and the bite metrics of [hunter-practice.md](hunter-practice.md) T20 to T23 (`eval.bite_wish_with_prey_in_jaws`, `eval.bite_wish_with_kin_in_jaws`, `eval.bite_wish_with_other_in_jaws`, `eval.bite_idle`) are the detectors of this phase and must exist before an arm is judged. For (c) the premise itself is unmeasured: E110 read mated hunters at 0.337 kills per 1,000 ticks against split hunters at 0.177 (n 9), the opposite of "mating loses the bite". T26 reads `eval.hunter_kill_rate_by_source.mate-hunter-parent` against `.split-hunter-parent` on the current defaults first, and (c) is refused without code if the mated rate is not below the split rate.

**Observation (what the code writes, checked on main).**

- A random genome of a `canHunt` specialisation gets, at `instinctWeight` 1, +1 on prey strength into `THRUST`, +1 on prey sine into `TURN` and +2 on prey strength into `BITE` (`applyHunterReflex` in `js/evo/instinct.js`, added on the random fill and clamped at `WEIGHT_CLAMP` 4), and a floor of `kinPullWeight` 0.5 x weight on kin strength into `THRUST` and kin sine into `TURN`.
- A child gets the same prey terms only on a flip into hunter (`applyBirthReflex` in `js/evo/policies/natural-birth.js`: a `canHunt` child of a mother that cannot hunt), written before mutation; every `canHunt` child gets the kin floor. A split child of a hunter copies its mother's row, so it keeps whatever the mother's weights now are.
- A mating child takes each skip column, one per output, whole from one parent (`crossSkip` in `js/evo/crossover.js`), so the prey cells for thrust, turn and bite sit in three different columns and come from independent draws: one parent can give the chase and the other the bite. `applyBirthReflex` runs after the crossover and adds nothing for a child of two hunters.
- No refusal exists. Kin strength into `BITE` and `other` strength into `BITE` are the random fill. The refusal the world does have is the price: `recoil` in `js/sim/feeding.js` charges `recoilEnergy` (20) to a hunter whose `BITE` is above 0 on a body `couldBite` refuses, and `agentChannel` shows kin on `kin` and a body it cannot take (and that cannot take it) on `other`.
- A body is too big only when `size[biter] > huntSizeRatio x size[victim]` fails. A forager's size is capped by its store at about 33, and a hunter of 31 at `huntSizeRatio` 0.8 bites up to 38.75, so almost nothing is too big in the live world, `recoilEnergy` is almost never charged for size and `other` rarely means "too big". A refusal learned only in a curriculum stage dies at export.
- `hebbianStep` changes only `fastOut`, never skip weights (What exists), and `rewardGain` 4, `rewardBase` 0.25 and `rewardDecay` 0.02 stay in `config-defaults.js`. A reward-gated arm cannot teach this reflex, and none is run.

**Theories.**

1. _The bite reflex has a yes and no no._ The prior says "bite prey" (+2) and nothing says "do not bite kin or what you cannot take". Nature: predators carry innate inhibitions as well as innate attacks (parents do not attack their young; a predator abandons prey it cannot take, [training.md](training.md) Phase 4), and the sign stimulus for the attack is specific to prey. Arm (a): wire the no at birth, where the prey term is.
2. _The world never makes the no matter._ With almost nothing too big, a refusal has nothing to refuse. Arm (b): one live arm where a too-big body exists, so the price is charged and the `other` sense carries meaning.
3. _A mating can keep the chase and lose the bite._ The three prey cells cross independently. Arm (c): a floor on the prey-into-bite cell for every hunter child, or the three prey cells crossed as one block. Only if the premise of the detector check holds.
