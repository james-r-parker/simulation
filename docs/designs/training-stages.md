A genome is trained by natural selection in whole worlds, as in [training.md](training.md). This doc changes how the worlds are ordered and how the result is judged. A stage is a world with one lesson. A stage ends when a gate of detectors says the lesson is learned, not on a generation count. Passed lessons are re-read at every checkpoint. Hunters and prey are read against each other's past, so an arms race is told from a cycle. An exam of ten rows says whether a champion set is good. Nothing here enters fitness, merit, mate rank or archive rank.

## Principle

- **A gate moves the world, never the score.** A gate decides when the next stage world starts. No offer, rank or birth reads a gate, a drill, a cross-play cell or an exam row ([principles.md](../principles.md), "Measure scenes, reward them almost never").
- **The curriculum changes the world, not the reward.** Every stage selects by survival and offspring under the live policy's fitness and proof rule.
- **Other blobs are the climate.** The lesson of a later stage is the other role's skill from an earlier one: prey evade hunters that hunt, hunters chase prey that evade.
- **A lesson counts when it is kept and inherited.** A passed gate that fails later is a finding. A champion whose descendants lose the skill has not learned it (selection rule 9).
- **Two customs survive export.** A champion set holds each specialisation's styles, not one best hunter ([principles.md](../principles.md), "Same spec, different custom").
- **One archive is a lead.** Every gate and exam verdict is read on 3 training-seed offsets and passes on at least 2.

## Gates

`js/evo/stage-gate.js` (new) exports `readGate(conditions, checkpoint)` and `gateState(history, conditions)`. Pure: no files, no clock.

| Part          | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Condition     | `{ "read": "<key>", "min": <n> }` or `{ "read": "<key>", "max": <n> }`. A key is a field of the checkpoint's gate readings: `drill.<name>.edgeLow`, `drill.<name>.winShareLow`, `drill.<name>.lossShare.<reason>`, `drill.<name>.winShareOverReference` (the win share's low bound over the `memoryless` reference's win share), `drill.pack-converge.gainLow`, `drill.pack-converge.blindGapLow`, `crossplay.hunterProgressLow`, `crossplay.preyProgressLow`, `crossplay.preyVsStage.<stage>`, `crossplay.cycleShare`, `retention.<stage>`, `retention.all` |
| Low bound     | A win share's low bound is `wilsonInterval` (`js/evo/binomial.js`) over the pooled trials of the champions the drill applies to. `edgeLow` is that low bound minus the high bound of the random brains' pooled win share. A gate checkpoint runs `GATE_TRIALS` (64) trials per champion and drill                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Pass          | Every condition holds. A `null` reading fails                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Hold          | A stage is passed when its gate passes on `GATE_HOLD` (2) checkpoints in a row and `minGenerations` have run                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Stall         | A required stage that reaches `maxGenerations` without passing stops the run: `TrainStageStalled`, exit code 3, the archive and curve kept. An optional stage that stalls logs `TrainStageStalled` with `optional` true, and the next stage starts. No required stage is skipped                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Retention     | Every checkpoint after `minGenerations` of the current stage re-reads the gate of each passed stage (so a big world jump like `pack` to `habitat` has time for `TRAINER_N_CAP` sample turnover before triggering a review). `retention.<stage>` is 1 when it passes, 0 when not; `retention.all` is 1 when every passed stage's is. A passed gate that fails on 2 checkpoints in a row logs `TrainStageRegressed`                                                                                                                                            |
| Review        | After `TrainStageRegressed` the trainer runs `REVIEW_GENERATIONS` (5) generations on the regressed stage's world, then returns to the current stage. A stage reviewed `REVIEW_MAX` (3) times stops the run as a stall                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| World metrics | The 4-seed checkpoint eval stays on the curve as columns. No gate condition reads an `eval.*` key: 4 seeds are under every metric's verdict count ([metrics.md](metrics.md))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Validity      | A gate is valid when the `memoryless` reference passes each of its drill conditions and the random brains fail each. T25 checks every gate of the ladder (`node scripts/ladder-validity.mjs`, which also tries `input-reader`, `kin-sparing` and the oracle where `memoryless` cannot play the drill); an invalid condition is rewritten or dropped with a Decisions row                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Determinism   | Gate readings come from seeded drills and cross-play, so the stage plan of a run is a function of its seeds, curriculum and offset. `--workers 1` and `--workers 32` write identical archives. Phase 8: no new stream; a screen run replays from `<runs>/<arm>/ladder.json`, its offset and the grid's trainer options, and a run reused by manifest is byte for byte the run it stands for                                                                                                                                                                  |

A curriculum entry gains `minGenerations`, `maxGenerations`, `gate`, `pool` and `checkpointEvery`. An entry with `generations` and no `gate` is a fixed-length stage, as today, so `evals/train-curriculum*.json` load unchanged. `planStages` lays out fixed stages; `nextStage(state, checkpoint)` in `js/evo/curriculum.js` (new) advances a gated run. The archive's `stages` record gains `passedAt`, `reviews` and the gate readings at the pass.

As built (Phase 2):

- `js/evo/stage-gate.js` holds `readGate`, `gateState`, `streaksOf`, `validateGate` and the constants `GATE_TRIALS`, `GATE_HOLD`, `REVIEW_GENERATIONS`, `REVIEW_MAX`. A bound (`min`, `max`) is inclusive, so "above 0" is a small positive `min`. `validateGate` runs when a curriculum loads and refuses an `eval.*` key or an unknown key.
- `js/evo/stage-run.js` holds the state machine; `js/evo/curriculum.js` re-exports `nextStage`, `initialState`, `checkpointDue`, `needsGate` and `stateAfter`. `nextStage(state, checkpoint)` is called after every generation and its checkpoint; the checkpoint is `{ readings }` or null. Every run uses it, a fixed-length curriculum being the machine with no gate, so fixed runs write archives and curve rows byte for byte as before (checked against `origin/main` on a `--config` run, a curriculum with checkpoints and a resume).
- A gated entry sets `gate`, `minGenerations` (default 1) and `maxGenerations` in place of `generations`; `optional`, `pool` and `checkpointEvery` apply to any entry. `pool` is validated and carried, and read by Phase 3. A gated stage needs checkpoints, so `--checkpoint-every 0` with no per-stage `checkpointEvery` is refused. The trainer forces a checkpoint at the generation where a gated stage would stall, so the stall reads the last gate.
- Retention is read only while a gated stage runs and only after its `minGenerations`; `retention.all` is 1 when no stage has passed. A regression starts a review, and a regression of a stage already reviewed `REVIEW_MAX` times stops the run as a stall. A review runs the regressed stage's world for `REVIEW_GENERATIONS` generations, takes no gate read, and returns to the current stage with its gate history cleared. The generations a review runs count in the reviewed stage's `generations` and not in the current stage's `minGenerations` or `maxGenerations`.
- A run that reaches `--generations` with its stage still open ends normally: `--generations` is the budget, `maxGenerations` the stall. `--until <stage>` cuts the curriculum after that stage; a gated last stage ends the run on its pass.
- Gate drills run on the validation seed (`GATE_SEED`, see "Seed partitions" below) with `GATE_TRIALS` trials per champion and drill, on every drill at once, so one drill table gives every stage's readings, and the curve's drill columns on a gate checkpoint come from the same table. A reading no Phase 3 or 4 code produces yet (`crossplay.*`, `drill.pack-converge.*`) is null and fails.
- A review that ends leaves a gated stage room for a hold before its stall (its generation count is cut to `maxGenerations` less `GATE_HOLD`). `retention.all` reads null before a stage's `minGenerations`, since retention is not read then; a drill's `lossShare` reads 0 for a reason that did not occur. A regression's checkpoint writes the failing conditions of the regressed stage to the curve row's gate column, marked `regressed`.
- `--resume` of a gated run continues from the archive's `trainState`; it is refused when the stage names or their `rulesHash` differ from the run's; an archive without it resumes a fixed-length curriculum (the state is replayed) and is refused by a gated one.
- Seed partitions, `manifest.json` and the export check are under "Runs are kept" below.

> Note: an elite keeps its samples when the stage world changes. Clone founders re-test it in the new world (`nextClone` in `js/evo/archive.js`) and `TRAINER_N_CAP` (8) bounds how long old samples weigh, so a lower bound drifts to the new world over a few generations. A gate read in the first checkpoint of a stage reads the old stage's elites.

## Cross-play

`js/evo/crossplay.js` (new) plays a predator genome against a forager genome in the staged arena of the `prey-ahead` drill, both driven by their brains. `playTrial` in `js/evo/memory-probe-run.js` gains an optional `opponent` (`Subject`) that drives the `prey` or `hunter` role in place of a controller.

| Part            | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Players         | Each checkpoint's champions: its predators and its foragers (one per specialisation until T20, one per style band after it)                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Cell            | `kill[i][j]`: the share of `CROSS_TRIALS` (64) trials in which checkpoint `i`'s predators kill checkpoint `j`'s foragers, on the same seeded plans (`fork(createRng(seed), 'crossplay')`) for every cell                                                                                                      |
| Hunter progress | The latest predators' mean kill share over all earlier forager columns, minus the first predators' mean over the same columns. `hunterProgressLow` is the latest mean's Wilson low bound minus the first mean's high bound, over the pooled trials                                                            |
| Prey progress   | The first foragers' mean kill share suffered over all earlier predator rows, minus the latest foragers' mean over the same rows, with `preyProgressLow` built the same way. `preyVsStage.<stage>` is the same difference against the hunters of the pool snapshot taken when `<stage>` passed, as a low bound |
| Cycle share     | Of the pairs of adjacent checkpoints, the share in which the newer side does worse than the older side against the other role's pooled past by more than 0.1                                                                                                                                                  |
| Group cell      | `packKill[i][j]`: as `kill`, with three sibling clones of the predator and a prey one bite cannot kill (the staging of `js/evo/memory-probe-group.js`)                                                                                                                                                        |
| Output          | `npm run train:crossplay -- <dir>` (`scripts/train-crossplay.mjs`) writes `<dir>/crossplay.json` and prints the matrix and the three numbers. The trainer computes the latest row and column at each checkpoint of a stage whose gate reads `crossplay.*`                                                     |

As built (Phase 3, T13 and T14):

- `playTrial` takes `opponent: { role: 'prey' | 'hunter', subject }`. Every staged agent with that role takes the opponent's specialisation and brain row, and the role's scripted controller is dropped (a reference subject's `act` drives it instead). A trial without an opponent is unchanged: a test pins the prey's position after 30 ticks to the value the fleer reached before the option existed.
- `js/evo/crossplay.js` holds `playCell`, `createMatrix`, `extendMatrix`, `hunterProgress`, `preyProgress`, `preyVsStageLow`, `cycleShare` and `crossplayNumbers`. Every cell runs the plans of `fork(createRng(1 + t), 'crossplay')` drawn for the nominal predator view, so a cell differs from another only in its two genomes. A pair of adjacent checkpoints counts toward the cycle share when either role's newer side is worse by more than 0.1. `huntPlan` and its arena rules are exported from `js/evo/memory-probe-group.js` for the group cell (T19 reuses them).
- `scripts/lib/train-crossplay.mjs` keeps the matrix over every checkpoint of the run. At the first gate checkpoint whose gate, or a passed stage's gate, reads `crossplay.*`, it reads the earlier checkpoints' champions from `curve.jsonl` and the `archive-<g>.json` files of the run directory, plays their cells, and from then on plays only the new row and column. A checkpoint whose archive holds no tested predator elite or forager elite adds no player, and the three numbers read null under two players, so the gate fails. `train:crossplay` reads the same files, plays the group cells too (`--no-group` skips them) and writes `crossplay.json`.
- `crossplay.preyVsStage.<stage>` is the low bound of the stage foragers' kill share suffered from the stage-pass snapshot's predators minus the same predators' share against the latest foragers (Phase 3's pool holds the snapshot; null when none is held).

## Opponent pool

`js/evo/opponent-pool.js` (new) keeps frozen archive snapshots (`snapshotArchive` in `js/evo/archive-snapshot.js`): one at each stage pass and one every `POOL_EVERY` (2) checkpoints, at most `POOL_MAX` (8), the stage-pass snapshots kept longest.

- In a stage with `pool` share `p`, each founder and floor entrant is drawn with probability `p` from a pool snapshot (uniform over snapshots) as an unmutated clone of an elite of the seeder's chosen specialisation, and otherwise from the frozen current archive as today. `createMapElitesV1` takes `opponents: { snapshots, share }`; `createSeeder` in `js/evo/policies/map-elites-seed.js` draws from `fork(rng, 'opponent-pool')`, so a share of 0 draws nothing and writes the archive it writes today.
- A pool founder is an opponent, not a candidate: the trainer drops an offer whose genome id is a pool founder's of that episode, so a genome the archive has replaced does not return through the pool (selection rule 1). Its children are new genomes born in this world, and their deaths are offered like any other.
- The pool is trainer state: it never reaches a champion file or the live world.

As built (Phase 3, T15):

- `js/evo/opponent-pool.js` is the pure bookkeeping: `addSnapshot` (over `POOL_MAX` the oldest periodic snapshot goes first, the oldest stage-pass one only when all are), `noteCheckpoint` (a stage pass takes a `stage-pass` snapshot, otherwise every `POOL_EVERY`-th checkpoint a `periodic` one), `stagePassSnapshot`, `describePool`. `scripts/lib/train-pool.mjs` takes the snapshots in the trainer, builds the `pool` an episode task carries and rebuilds a pool on `--resume`.
- A run keeps a pool only when a stage has a `pool` share above 0; its archive files then record `pool` (`checkpoints` and each snapshot's generation, stage and kind), and a resume rebuilds the snapshots from the `archive-<g>.json` files beside the resumed one and refuses a missing file. A task carries `pool` only for a stage with a share above 0 and a non-empty pool, so a share of 0 or none writes the archives and files a run without the option writes (tested byte for byte).
- The draw is `createPoolDraw` in `js/evo/policies/map-elites-pool.js`. It runs in `initBrain` after `initSpec` has chosen the specialisation: a draw from `fork(rng, 'opponent-pool')` below the share picks a uniform snapshot and its `nextClone` of that specialisation; a snapshot with no elite of it falls back to the ordinary draw. A pool founder carries no clone marks, logs `ImmigrantSeeded` with source `pool` and has its genome id in `policy.poolFounders`; `createOfferSink` in `js/evo/trainer.js` drops the offers of those ids, and a founder's children are offered as usual.

As built (Phase 4, T17 to T19):

- `DRILLS` lists eleven drills: the seven of [training.md](training.md), then `SOCIAL_DRILLS` of `js/evo/drill-scenarios-social.js` in the order of the table. - `prey-crossing` is `preyDrill` with the prey's starting heading 60 to 120 degrees off the subject's (its place is as in `prey-ahead`: 0.6 of the view, within 45 degrees of the heading). `hunter-flank` stages the hunter 500 px away, 90 to 150 degrees off the forager's heading and facing it.
- `kin-spared` stages the subject and a sibling with one shared parent id, the sibling 40 px away on the side opposite the prey (45 degrees off the heading, inside the jaws, whose half-angle is 90) and the prey as in `prey-ahead`. The sibling is an idle agent of role `sibling` (slot 2). `js/evo/drill-kin-trace.js` counts the ticks on which the sibling touches the subject inside its jaws and `BITE` is above 0: the sim already refuses a bite on kin (`couldBite`), so the drill reads the brain's wish. A lost trial with any such tick reads the loss reason `bit-kin` (`LOST_REASONS` in `js/evo/gate-readings.js`), else the chase's own reason. The scripted references (`memoryless`, `input-reader`) bite whenever prey is in view, so they lose this drill; the oracle that bites only a touching prey wins it.
- `pack-converge` is `huntPlan` of `js/evo/memory-probe-group.js` (already exported by Phase 3) with the trio as the drill's own trials, under the probe's arena rules (`arena`: `wallDamage` 0). The drill report prints `solo`, `blind`, `gain [low]` and `gap [low]` in the cell, and the gate readings of the drill carry `gainLow` and `blindGapLow` pooled over the champions (null when an arm was not played).
- The scripted hunters carry no pack: on 16 trials `memoryless` and `input-reader` win 0.94 and 1.0 of the trio trials and the same of the solo and kin-blind arms (a gain and gap of 0), and the oracle that sends every hunter at the prey wins 0.5 as a trio, 0.5 kin-blind and 1.0 alone (three bodies crowd one prey). - `bench-reflex` keeps timing the five forager drills of the original seven, so `bench.reflex_vs_drill` stays comparable.

## Export by style

`exportBestPerStyle` in `js/evo/champion-export.js` (new) replaces `exportBestPerSpec` as the export of `train:export` and the checkpoint. For each specialisation it takes the tested elite with the highest lower bound in each of `STYLE_BANDS` (2) halves of the style axis of `descriptorV7` (`js/evo/descriptors.js`): for a hunter, short chases and long ones. A band with no tested elite gives no champion. A champion file gains `style` (`low` or `high`); `validateChampion` in `js/evo/league.js` accepts and ignores it. `exportBestPerSpec`, `bestElitePerSpec` and their uses are replaced in the same task.

As built (T20, T20a):

- `STYLE_BANDS` (2), `styleBandOf(cell)` (the row of the cell, the style axis, split at half), `bestElitePerStyle(archive)` (per specialisation and band, the tested elite of the highest lower bound, with the band and the bound) and `exportBestPerStyle` are in `js/evo/champion-export.js`; `exportBestPerSpec` and `bestElitePerSpec` are deleted. A file gains `style` (`low` or `high`); `validateChampion` already ignores unknown fields, a test pins that a file with `style` loads. `train:export` writes up to two files per specialisation and each index row names its style; `eliteSubjects` (`scripts/lib/drill-subjects.mjs`, which the checkpoint, `drill --archive` and `reflex --archive` use) returns the same picks with `style` and `lowerBound`, so the drill table of a gate checkpoint pools both predator bands. `playerOf` of the cross-play keeps one predator and one forager per checkpoint: the one of the highest bound, whichever band (cross-play by style band is not built). The skip-cell table of `train:export` covers every exported predator champion.
- `js/evo/style-evidence.js` is the evidence the exam's row 8 will read (wiring is T23b): `matchedTrials` plays `prey-ahead` for each band's champion on one seed (so trial `i` is the same staging for both), `pairedSeparation` reads the paired difference of a trace measure (`biteTicks`, the chase's length in biting ticks) with a 95% normal interval and says `separated` only from 16 pairs and an interval that excludes 0, and `styleVerdict` says `viable` only when both bands are filled and beat the random brains (`edgeLow` and `winShareLow` above 0: a short chase with no catch is not an ambush), the bands separate on matched trials, and the descendant recruitment reading of each band is `true` (an unread reading is `null` and fails).

## Release competence contracts

These additions extend the existing ladder and exam; their reasoning is in [training-research.md](training-research.md#release-competence-review). They add no fitness, merit or mating reward. Recruitment instrumentation comes from [honest-budgets.md](honest-budgets.md) Phase 6; reuse it rather than adding a second lineage ledger.

### A complete hunter life

Insert required `hunt-life` between `hunt` and `evade`, with 10 to 60 generations and the existing two-checkpoint hold. Its world uses target birth, maturity, diet and energy rules, no floor or reseeding, and enough space for unsuccessful searches as well as encounters. Initial adults receive the normal founder budget; offspring receive only the normal parental gift. Charge all failed pursuit, bite, movement and maintenance costs. No kill, birth or juvenile ration is scripted.

The gate uses a dedicated seeded life-cycle evaluation, not the four-seed checkpoint eval columns. Register its readings alongside the existing gate keys and test null and insufficient-family cases as failures. The gate reports the share of founder families with a deliberate hunter kill, a birth and a child reaching maturity, plus net hunting income and juvenile failure causes. T25a fixes the observation horizon, family denominator, minimum independent episodes and pass threshold before the first ladder run, using competent and incompetent controls. No calibrated threshold means the gate is invalid, not passed. A required stage that stalls stops normally. This stage and dependent ladder runs wait for the recruitment diagnostics and a viable world budget; tool implementation can proceed earlier. Two descendant generations are required by the release exam, not by this introductory stage.

### Training, validation and sealed release worlds

T10a assigns disjoint seed namespaces and scenario manifests to training, checkpoint validation and release. Checkpoint gates, reviews and curriculum tuning read validation only. Release seeds and scenarios never select a checkpoint or offset. Freeze the candidate set, founder mixture, code, rules hash and curriculum hash before opening the release exam. A failed exam remains in the record; a revised candidate uses a new, precommitted release partition. Log every partition use, including resume, and refuse overlap. Do not claim that a repeatedly inspected exam remains held out.

Validation and release vary headings, spacing, food placement, density and starting seasonal phase within a declared target-world envelope. They do not change physical rules to manufacture success. Record exact seeds and scenario hashes for replay. Use paired worlds for comparisons; report uncertainty across independent world seeds and training runs, not just pooled trials from related clones. Two checkpoint passes are an advancement rule, not independent replication evidence.

### Inheritance follows families

Exam row 7 uses traced descendants of the released founders, not archive elites as a substitute. Track parentage through mating, splitting and role changes using stable identities; mixed ancestry is explicit and never counted twice in aggregate totals. Report all founder families, including those with no children, extinct lines and children still immature at the observation boundary. A fixed-seed sample of ordinary children and grandchildren supplies the descendant drills; best descendants are a separate diagnostic. Reset recurrent and temporary learned state through the normal newborn path before each drill.

For each drill, each descendant generation must have a win share at least half the founders' share and a low bound above the random brains' high bound, applied separately to children and grandchildren. It additionally requires evidence of descendant reproduction without rescue and the predeclared family-replacement bar from T23a. Missing descendants cannot pass through a smaller surviving denominator. Run for at least two complete seasonal cycles; unfinished generations are censored and cannot establish a pass. Report ordinary-descendant results, extinction and reproductive replacement together. Do not pool offsets to hide a failed run.

### Opponents and exported populations

Keep the simple forager `prey-ahead` cross-play matrix as a diagnostic. Add a release matrix across every prey specialisation, juvenile and mature bodies, cover, sparse encounters and depleted patches. Opponents include independently trained runs excluded from the candidate's training pool. Record outcomes per guild and condition, including failed pursuit cost. Hunters need viable opportunities in the mixed world; beating every defended prey is not a requirement.

The two style bands generate export candidates, not proof of two strategies. Exam row 8 also requires repeatable behavioural separation in matched scenes and viable descendant recruitment for both hunter styles. A short chase with no catch is not an ambush. Compare styles alone and in the intended release mixture, with matched total starting population and energy. Freeze founder counts, age distribution, spatial arrangement and kin assignment in the release manifest. Test the exact exported mixture under row 6; abundance of one style cannot conceal extinction of the other. No role quota or replacement injection maintains diversity.

As built (T14a): `js/evo/crossplay-release.js` plays one predator against opponents of every guild (the opponent's specialisation) in `AGES` (`juvenile`, `mature`) and `HABITATS` (`open`, `cover`, `sparse`, `depleted`) and records, per opponent and condition, the kills, the trials and the mean energy the predator lost in the trials that did not kill (negative when it scavenged), tagged with the opponent's provenance. `cover` rings the prey in rocks, `sparse` stages the prey at the edge of the predator's sight, `depleted` gives the predator the least energy a staged agent holds, and a juvenile is a prey at 0.6 of the base size (the staged world has no ageing, so size is the body proxy). `independence(candidate, opponent)` calls an opponent independent only when its run, training-seed offset and the runs of the candidate's pool all differ from the candidate's; `reportByCondition` pools rows by guild, age, habitat or independence with Wilson bounds. It is a report with no bar. Wiring it to the exam rows and to trained opponents' manifests is T23b.

### Stage-local archive evidence

T9a built the provenance and eligibility half: the archive's `stages` record and `rulesHash` survive snapshots and resume (tested through a resume to a byte-identical archive), and `exportEligibility` (`js/evo/export-eligibility.js`) says an archive's champions may be published only when its last generation, a review's included, ran under the target rules. The comparison below is T9b, which needs ladder runs, so it sits in Phase 6. T9b compares retained bounded samples with stage-local estimates on matched training seeds. Both arms carry the same genomes; the fresh-estimate arm preserves old observations for diagnostics but excludes their scores from current-stage ranking until re-evaluated. Record candidate turnover, retention, sample cost and later gate outcomes. Choose the scoring rule on validation data before release evaluation. Irrespective of that choice, an export candidate needs recorded evaluation under the target rules; an easy-stage score alone cannot qualify it. Stage and rules identifiers survive snapshots and resume, and worker counts produce identical results.

## Runs are kept

- A run writes to `.sim/train/<name>/` (git-ignored): `archive-<g>.json`, `curve.jsonl`, `train.jsonl`, `crossplay.json`, `exam.json` and `manifest.json` (the git sha, the command line, the curriculum file's hash, the seed offset, each stage's `rulesHash`, `passedAt` and gate readings).
- `main` in `scripts/train.mjs` (the command line, not a call to `runTraining`) refuses an `--out` under `/tmp` unless `--scratch` is given.
- A run a decision rests on is committed under `evals/decisions/<id>/`: the last archive, the curve, the cross-play matrix, the exam and the manifest.

### Seed partitions

`js/evo/partitions.js` (T10a) gives training, validation and each release its own range of one million seeds: training `[0, 1e6)` (`evals/train-seeds.json`, `evals/seeds.json` and the curve drills' seed 1), validation `[1e6, 2e6)`, `release-<n>` after it. `partitionManifest` records a partition's first seed, how many it uses and a hash of the scenarios they run; `assertDisjoint` refuses overlap. A gate checkpoint runs its drills on the first validation seed (`GATE_SEED`) and nothing in the training namespace picks a checkpoint or a gate.

- `recordAccess` is the ledger: a purpose reads only its own kind (`train` and `resume` read training, `gate`, `review` and `tune` read validation, `exam` reads release), and an `exam` opens a release partition only with a `freezeCandidate` hash (the champion set, the code sha, the rules hash, the curriculum hash and the founder mixture). A release partition opened for one candidate refuses another: a failed exam stays in the record and a revised candidate uses a new, precommitted `release-<n>`.
- `manifest.json` carries the partition manifests and every use: the training use, each gate checkpoint, and a resume (the earlier uses are read from the resumed archive's directory), each also logged as `PartitionUse`. The exam (Phase 5) is the one caller of `exam` and of `freezeCandidate`; the validation and release scenario envelopes (headings, spacing, food, density, seasonal phase) are Phase 5 work and use `partitionManifest`.
- A run's result does not depend on the worker count: the gate seed and its trials are fixed, so `--workers 1` and `--workers 2` write identical archives and manifests (tested).

### Phase 9 - Co-evolved rungs

**Targets:** none - a training reading: the `hunt` rung read by cross-play against an evolving prey population, and the cycle share of `npm run train:crossplay`

Every rung above `nursery` trains hunters against prey that a script or a frozen genome drives. A fitness that can be solved against a fixed world is solved, and then the picture stops changing (`docs/principles.md`, "Other blobs are the climate"). This phase starts only after [fair-chase.md](fair-chase.md) Phase 3 adopts: on a chase the bodies cannot win, an evolving prey only widens the gap, and the opponent pool of T61 read no gain there (E204).

- In a co-evolved stage the prey are a second population with its own archive, trained in the same episodes on its own fitness (surviving the hunters while feeding).
- The stage's gate reads cross-play, not a drill against a scripted fleer: the current hunter champions against the prey champions of the last 5 checkpoints, and the current prey champions against the hunters of the last 5. A stage passes when hunters win a share inside the fair band (0.2 to 0.8) against current prey and the earlier-checkpoint hunters win less than the current ones against current prey.
- A cycle (hunters that beat old prey and lose to older prey) is read by the cycle share of `train:crossplay`; above 0.3 the stage adds the earlier champions to the opponent pool.
- The scripted rungs stay as the way in: `nursery` to `hunt-mid` teach the turn, the contact and the bite before the prey starts to learn.
- The exam's cross-play row and the champion pool ([champion-pool.md](champion-pool.md)) read the co-evolved champions unchanged.
